## Three Acceptable Visual Directions

### 1. Recommended: Utah Event Authority Studio

This direction is the best fit for the current brand reset.

Interface tone:

- Calm, high-trust, practical, and event-business oriented.
- The customer feels they are planning with an experienced Utah decor company, not playing with a toy.
- Copy stays plain: "Choose a piece", "Match your colors", "Add another piece", "Send this plan to Locally Twisted".

Color and material direction:

- Base: warm white, soft paper, ink black, deep slate, restrained blue, muted berry, and small gold/brass accents.
- Avoid pastel backgrounds as the default shell. Pastels belong in customer-selected balloon palettes, not the company frame.
- Use slate/blue/berry photo treatment for proof images and preview scenes where color grading is needed.
- Use black and gold sparingly for completion, quote-readiness, and saved-plan surfaces.

Rendering style:

- Use clean, semi-realistic balloon forms with visible cluster rhythm, subtle highlights, and consistent shadows.
- Show scale through simple venue-ground planes, doorway/entry frames, stage lines, table silhouettes, human silhouettes, or school/civic context cues.
- Use mountain or Utah territory cues as subtle framing, not decorative wallpaper.

Why recommend it:

- It directly supports the premium/civic/professional direction in `workstreams/brand-audience-style-reset.md`.
- It preserves interactivity while making the studio feel like a serious planning surface for corporate, school, civic, venue, and premium private events.
- It leaves room for family-party warmth without letting the main company identity become a party-store palette.

### 2. Portfolio-Led Planning Board

This direction makes real installation proof the visual anchor.

Interface tone:

- Gallery-first, calm, and consultative.
- The customer starts from event contexts and real work examples, then builds a plan from proven pieces.

Color and material direction:

- Neutral gallery base: white, charcoal, slate, light grey, and one Locally Twisted accent.
- The strongest color comes from photos and balloon choices.
- UI controls should feel like portfolio filters and planning tools, not product cards.

Rendering style:

- Pair every generated/SVG preview with a nearby real-install reference where available.
- Canvas output can sit on simple photo-derived stage, gym, or venue backgrounds if source-approved.
- Avoid claiming a generated rendering is proof of completed work.

Best use:

- Strong for high-trust buyers who want evidence before customization.
- Useful if GL wants the future studio to bridge from portfolio proof into inquiry.

Tradeoff:

- Requires more image selection, source logging, and asset QA. The brand reset notes that v1 photo quality is uneven, so this direction depends on careful curation.

### 3. Civic Proposal Builder

This direction treats the studio as an early proposal tool for organizations and stakeholders.

Interface tone:

- Structured, executive, and approval-friendly.
- Feels like "build a visual brief for our event" rather than "design a party decoration".

Color and material direction:

- Base: off-white, dark slate, navy, civic blue, restrained berry, and brass/gold for status.
- Stronger use of tables, summaries, and "plan" artifacts.
- Customer palette controls are clear and named, with color swatches secondary to readable color names.

Rendering style:

- Main preview shows the installation in a clean scale scene.
- Side panel summarizes pieces, colors, dimensions, context, and "pieces considered".
- Completion output should look like a shareable design brief or proposal cover, not a checkout page.

Best use:

- Excellent for corporate, school, city, church, venue, and stakeholder approval workflows.
- Strong match for future save/share behavior.

Tradeoff:

- Can feel too formal for premium private events unless softened with photography and friendlier empty states.

## What To Keep From The Contest Renders

- Keep C4's visual thumbnails inside style buttons, but redraw them in a more professional style with fewer toy-like outlines.
- Keep C2's pre-tinted cascading suggestion mechanic. It is the strongest way to make multi-piece planning feel natural without pressuring the customer.
- Keep C3's dual-audience completion card. The final plan should serve both the customer and Locally Twisted's sales follow-up.
- Keep C1's "pieces considered" payload, but keep it visually quiet for customers and operationally clear in the backend summary.
- Keep the color-name emphasis from C3's picker and the product-details rule. Color names are operationally load-bearing.
- Keep "permission to ignore" behavior for suggested pieces. Suggestions should feel helpful, not like upsell pressure.

## What To Reject From The Contest Renders

- Reject rainbow/pastel-first brand framing as the main company identity.
- Reject large rounded toy cards as the default design language.
- Reject childish "coloring book" framing in customer-facing naming, even if some coloring mechanics survive internally.
- Reject renders where balloons are simple same-size dots without construction logic.
- Reject "Send this to Jeff" as public-facing final language. Use "Send this plan to Locally Twisted" or "Talk with Locally Twisted" unless GL deliberately chooses a founder-specific moment.
- Reject ecommerce cues that imply exact pricing, instant checkout, or guaranteed engineering accuracy for custom installs.

## Premium But Interactive

The studio can stay approachable without becoming childish by using these rules:

- Use large, obvious controls for core choices, but style them like planning controls rather than toy tiles.
- Let the first interaction be visual and low-risk: choose event context or starting piece.
- Make suggested additions palette-aware, optional, and easy to dismiss.
- Show customer progress through the plan, not through gamified badges.
- Use plain labels: "Entrance arch", "Backdrop and garland", "Pair of columns", "Balloon drop".
- Use a stable right or bottom summary panel for selected pieces, named colors, scale, and next action.
- Keep the CTA consultative: "Send plan", "Share plan", "Ask about this setup", "Talk through details".

## Visual Rules For Image, Canvas, And SVG Output

Balloon construction:

- Classic arches and columns must show repeatable 4-balloon cluster rhythm or a believable simplified equivalent.
- Backdrops should read as a cluster grid or intentional large regions, not free-floating circles.
- Organic garlands should use mixed balloon sizes, controlled randomness, and no identical same-size color touching when possible.
- Balloon drops should be representational color mixes, not patterned designs that gravity would destroy.

Scale and scene:

- Every customer-facing preview should include at least one scale cue once a piece is selected.
- Acceptable scale cues include doorway frames, tables, stage edges, gym/civic room floor lines, human silhouettes, vehicles for parade/public installs, or venue wall/floor boundaries.
- Do not use decorative mountain art in a way that competes with the event preview. Use territory cues as subtle frame, texture, or photo treatment.

Color:

- Keep UI chrome neutral enough that selected balloon colors remain legible.
- Show balloon color names beside selected swatches and in the final summary.
- Do not certify brand/Pantone matching until Jeff-approved mappings exist.
- Use hex approximations only as visual aids and mark them as approximate in internal payloads.

Output:

- The final share/send screen should read like a clean event plan: selected pieces, named colors, approximate scale, event context, optional notes, and pieces considered.
- The output should explicitly imply "planning visualization", not final engineering drawing.
- Generated or illustrated previews must never be presented as completed-install proof.

## Three Visual Failure Modes To Avoid

1. Party-store configurator.
   Bright yellow/blue retail UI, price-first cards, add-to-cart framing, and coupon energy make custom installations feel like commodity products.

2. Kids' coloring game.
   Oversized pastel cards, cartoon balloons, rainbow headers, playful stickers, and "coloring book" language undercut the professional/civic buyer strategy.

3. Fake engineering blueprint.
   Over-precise renderings, exact quantities, or AR-style claims can imply installation guarantees the planning tool cannot safely make.

## Key Decisions

- Recommended visual direction: Utah Event Authority Studio.
- Use "Plan Custom Decor" as the customer-facing surface name unless GL chooses another final label.
- Treat "Design Studio" as an internal/workstream label or secondary product label, not necessarily the first customer-facing phrase.
- The UI shell should be professional and restrained; balloon colors and real work provide the joy.
- Use Locally Twisted as the brand in customer-facing language. Jeff can appear in internal notes and operational handoff, but not as the customer-facing brand center.
- Separate balloon decor construction from balloon twisting. Twisting can stay in the broader company/service ecosystem, but the studio's visual rules should focus on decor installations.
- Prefer Frappe-native friendly output: SVG/canvas with structured data, not a separate game-like app or heavy external runtime unless approved later.

## Risks Or Contradictions

- The contest renders prove useful mechanics, but their visual shell conflicts with the newer premium/civic brand reset.
- The contest included bouquet and centerpiece concepts, but current V2 framing should not force every customizable SKU into the studio. Smaller ready-to-order items belong elsewhere.
- The physics reference render is more structurally believable than the contest icons, but its rough 3D ground/lighting is not yet premium enough for customer-facing use.
- Black/gold professionalism could work, but too much black/gold can feel luxury-event-only and may narrow the civic/school/community appeal.
- Mountain/Utah cues can support local authority, but overusing them could make the studio feel like a tourism brand instead of an event planning tool.
- Current brand reset is still a working surface, not the final style guide. This file should feed synthesis, not override the current launch guide.
