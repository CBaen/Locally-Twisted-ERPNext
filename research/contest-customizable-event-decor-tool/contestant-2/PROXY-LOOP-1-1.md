## Research Quality Probe

Your research is solid and you've found sources that others missed. PilaMania (pilamania.com/en/products/3d-color-designs/) is the most valuable competitive find in the field — a live balloon tool that actually uses the inquiry-not-checkout model with "you decide when your request is ready." That source does real work in your reasoning. Your three-component color picker (recents + tabs + hex) is grounded across multiple citations and the components fit together coherently. Three places I want you to look more carefully.

---

**Probe 1 — Fanfaire SWAP mechanic, and "inverting" it**

In REASONING Q5, you describe your cascading ghost upsell as drawing from "Fanfaire's SWAP mechanic but inverted: instead of swapping, we're offering the next natural complement." The citation is https://www.fanfaire.io/design-studio.

Summarize in your own words what the SWAP feature at that URL actually does. Is it a customer-facing action (the customer presses SWAP and gets an alternative)? Or is it a design-studio workflow feature for the decorator (the decorator swaps pieces in a proposal before sending to the client)? Understanding the actual direction of the SWAP mechanic matters: if it's decorator-facing, your "inversion" is more of an invention — you're applying the conceptual shape of the mechanic in a new direction. That's not a problem, but framing it as your own move ("inspired by the concept of substitution") is more honest than "inverting SWAP," which implies the customer-facing pattern already exists and you're flipping it.

Also: your ghost upsell is described as cascading — color the arch, ghost column appears; color the column, ghost backdrop appears. That's a distinct and interesting choice. Does anything in your research support cascading as better than showing all ghosts at once? If not, be explicit that it's a design judgment, not a researched pattern.

---

**Probe 2 — UXPin "8-10 per group" specifically**

Your Q3 answer states 6 hue-family tabs of ~8-10 swatches as the right chunk size, citing https://www.uxpin.com/create-design-system-guide/build-color-palette-for-design-system.

Summarize what the UXPin article actually says about group size. Does it state "8-10 per group" as a specific guideline, or does it describe general principles about organizing large color palettes (families, tiers, etc.) without naming a specific count? Your RESEARCH-NOTES entry says "10-11 swatches per 'family group' is the recommended chunk size" — that's specific enough to be worth confirming is in the source vs. your interpretation of it. If the source doesn't give a specific number, the design choice (6 tabs × ~8-10) is still sound, but it should be presented as your reasoned estimate rather than a research-backed spec.

---

**Probe 3 — DIY Candy / Recolor "swipe from bottom"**

Your bottom-sheet justification (Q1 and Q3) cites "Recolor's 'swipe from bottom' mechanic" at https://diycandy.com/best-adult-coloring-apps/.

Summarize what that source actually says about Recolor's color picker interaction. Is the "swipe from bottom" a description of how Recolor's color picker opens — as a documented UX pattern that you're borrowing? Or is it mentioned more briefly, as one of several features in a list? A bottom sheet for color picking is widely used on mobile regardless of whether this particular article documents it explicitly — so the design choice is well-grounded on its own. But if the article only mentions the Recolor interaction in passing, your citation is doing more work than the source supports. Be honest about whether this is a verified pattern from the source or a standard mobile pattern that you've correctly applied.

---

**Push toward better**

The cascading ghost upsell is your most distinctive move and it's not fully defended anywhere in your reasoning. You mention it as the mechanic and you cite the SWAP inversion, but you don't argue *why cascading* is better than showing two ghosts at once. Consider: does cascading risk losing a customer who came for a backdrop (not an arch) and has to color through three shapes to get the ghost they actually want? Or does the sequence of completion create momentum — each colored shape unlocking the next? Lean into that argument explicitly. It's a strong design choice but it needs more than "this draws from SWAP." The cascade is yours. Own it.
