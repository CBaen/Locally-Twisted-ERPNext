**Probe 1 — Color inheritance and Source 14 / Source 4**

Your discovery upsell mechanic (REASONING Q5) describes suggestion chips that show the suggested piece *already rendered in the customer's chosen palette* — the coral arch chip in the customer's coral-and-gold colors, ready to confirm. You cite Source 14 (Fanfaire variants) and Source 4 (Gemar Creator presets) as the seeds for this.

Tell me, in your own words, what Fanfaire's "variants" feature actually does at https://www.fanfaire.io/design-studio. Does it show an alternative piece pre-rendered in the customer's *current* color selection? Or does it show design alternatives from the studio's own preset palette? The distinction matters: if Fanfaire's variants show Jeff's pre-made design options (not the customer's live palette projected forward), then the color-inheritance leap is yours — which is fine, but it should be framed as your invention, not as "a pattern from the research." Same question for Gemar Creator presets: the presets are starting points that customers then modify, not a system where a suggested next piece inherits a customer's in-progress choices. Honest framing here will make your reasoning stronger, not weaker — it's a genuinely good idea that doesn't need a citation to justify it.

**Probe 2 — Source 7, Pigment tap-region-to-activate**

You write: "The Pigment coloring app (Source 7) proves the 'tap a region to activate it' mechanic is intuitive." This is load-bearing for your entire Q1 answer.

The URL is https://www.idownloadblog.com/2016/01/05/pigment-review/ — a 2016 iDownloadBlog review.

Summarize in your own words what that review specifically describes about Pigment's region-activation interaction. Does it describe a "tap a region to activate it, then color inside that region" two-step mechanic? If the latter, your two-step is still defensible — but from a design choice you're making, not a citation you're citing.
