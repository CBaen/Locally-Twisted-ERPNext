## Probe 2 — DesignFiles and horizontal scroll

**What the source actually contains:** The DesignFiles article (blog.designfiles.co/moodboard-apps/) describes app features — background removal tools, templates, collaboration features — across Moodboard, Milanote, Shuffles, and Morpholio Board. It does not compare horizontal scroll vs. vertical scroll vs. freeform canvas. It does not make a claim that horizontal outperforms other layout directions. It describes Shuffles as feeling "like digital scrapbooking" with layering and animation, and mentions Moodboard's "drag-and-drop layout builder" and Milanote's "flexible layout" — but none of this is a comparative statement about scroll direction.

**Verdict: Citation reaches.** My RESEARCH-NOTES entry stated "Horizontal row (scroll right) works better on mobile than a 2D free canvas" as if the DesignFiles article established this. It didn't. The horizontal scroll is my design judgment, not a finding.

**What actually supports horizontal scroll:** The judgment is sound but rests on reasoning, not the citation:
- A free 2D canvas on mobile invites accidental drag (touch distinguishes scroll from drag only with careful implementation)
- Horizontal scroll maps to natural thumb movement and the "spread" / "book pages" metaphor in the brief
- Each piece being discrete and side-by-side matches how a customer would lay physical balloon props across a venue wall for preview

**Action taken:** Rewrote the Q2 citation in REASONING.md to attribute the horizontal scroll choice to my design reasoning (informed by the "scrapbook feel" described in moodboard app research, not claimed by it). The DesignFiles article is still cited for the "scrapbooking vs. design tool process feel" distinction — which it does make. RESEARCH-NOTES updated to reflect what the article actually says vs. what I inferred.
