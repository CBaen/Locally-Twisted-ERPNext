## Utah Balloon Creations
**URL:** https://utahballooncreations.com/
**What they do:** Custom balloon decor for corporate events, schools, social events, and seasonal installations. Deliver and install pre-inflated; serve all of Utah.

**Homepage shape (first ~800px):** A booking urgency banner ("SPRING DATES ARE FILLING FAST — BOOK TODAY") sits at the top. The page immediately drops into a "LET'S GET STARTED!" section offering two booking pathways: custom quote request, or quick-order (listed as "coming soon"). No hero portfolio image above the fold.

**Navigation (in order):** Corporate Events, Schools, Social Events, Seasonal, Shop, Info (dropdown: Pricing, Service Areas, Color Chart, About Us, FAQ, Environment, Blog, Contact)

**Portfolio treatment:** No traditional gallery or portfolio section. The site is organized by event category (corporate, schools, social), not by visual work. Browsing implies knowing what you need rather than being shown what's possible.

**Shop presence:** Quick-order section is advertised but marked "coming soon." Not functional at time of visit.

**Custom inquiry handling:** "Quick quote request" form — submit event details, receive a proposal, pay invoice, then install.

**Candid note:** Booking-machine structure — efficient for repeat customers who already know what they want, but offers no visual inspiration to a first-time buyer. Portfolio is implicit (category browsing) rather than explicit (gallery). Misses the aspirational/showroom moment entirely.

## The Balloon Loft
**URL:** https://theballoonloft.com/
**What they do:** "Balloon event stylists + premium party store" — serves all of Utah from American Fork. Installations plus a physical retail store selling party supplies.

**Homepage shape (first ~800px):** Large banner image with "Let's Party!" tagline and a "Hire us!" CTA button. Clean, energetic.

**Navigation (in order):** Balloons, Installation, Grab & Go, DIY Kit, Helium, Backdrops, Party & Event Planning, Hire Us, Shop, Contact, Blog, Login Account. Shopping cart in header.

**Portfolio treatment:** Three "Fan Favorites" cards below the fold (Balloon Installations, Backdrops, Party Supplies) with images. Modest visual portfolio — shows categories, not specific event case studies.

**Shop presence:** Prominent — dedicated Shop section in navigation plus cart icon. 50+ party supply SKUs visible ($5–$18 range). Products include tableware, toppers, candles, DIY kits, themed party supplies (branded: Meri Meri, My Mind's Eye). DIY balloon garlands from $30. This is a significant revenue line, not a sidebar.

**Custom inquiry handling:** "Hire Us" form on its own page. Can also visit the physical store.

**Candid note:** The only site in this survey with a genuinely active, well-stocked retail shop alongside custom installation services. Useful as a reference for what "shop + service" looks like when both are real — the shop here is a full party-supply store, not a curated sub-$200 add-on basket.

## Patterns Observed

1. **No custom inquiry, no custom shop.** Every site except The Balloon Loft routes its high-dollar custom work through a consultation/quote request form — not a price list, not a configurator. The high-touch model is nearly universal in this category. E-commerce exists only where the product is pre-configured and sub-$30.

2. **Portfolio is a navigation item, not a homepage feature.** Most sites name "Portfolio," "Gallery," or "Our Work" as a top-level nav item, but only embed a teaser (3–5 images or a carousel) on the homepage. The full work lives one click away. Letting the preview do the seduction, not the catalog, is the dominant pattern.

3. **The shop, when it exists, is a sidebar — not the headline.** Unwritten Florals carries a shop link; The Balloon Loft runs a full party-supply store. In both cases the homepage foregrounds the custom/service identity and the shop is accessible without being promoted. No site in this survey leads with its shop as the primary identity.

4. **"Inquire" beats "Buy" at every price point above ~$30.** Even sites with a shop page separate the custom engagement from the retail engagement with entirely different CTAs, flows, and page structures. The consultation funnel and the cart funnel are never mixed.

5. **Social proof format matches the tier.** Entry-level Utah competitors (Utah Balloon Creations, The Balloon Loft) rely on customer testimonials. Mid-tier operators (Partistry, Atlanta Balloon Designer) embed Google reviews. Top-tier studios (Amaryllis, Unwritten Florals) use press features and editorial publication logos. The most aspirational sites show the fewest explicit reviews — the portfolio speaks for itself.
