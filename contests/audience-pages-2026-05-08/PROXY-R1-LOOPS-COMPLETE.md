## Field-Wide Patterns

**The proof-density problem is structural, not content-level.** Every contestant brought real clients, real photos, and honest numbers. No fabricated claims were detected. The gap in the field is not what proof is present — it is where proof is placed and what form it takes. Several suites carry strong credentials in their named-client grids but let those grids operate as scannable name walls rather than calibrated signals. The distinction matters: a marketing director scanning for peer-industry clients and a civic coordinator scanning for peer cities need different organizational logic applied to the same data. Contestants who understood this (sector groupings, category labels, honest short-roster framing) are further ahead of those who displayed the same data in a flat list and trusted the names to do all the work.

**The memorial/celebration-of-life moment is the field's most revealing divergence.** Every contestant addressed it — but at very different depths. Some buried it as a bullet inside a category card. Some gave it a named section with full prose. Some placed it contextually adjacent to the most powerful testimonial in the review set (KJSCOTT). The page that earns a grieving person's trust before they decide whether to reach out is not the one that mentions memorial services — it is the one that makes them feel found before they have to look for themselves. The field has strong work here, but the highest bar has not yet been fully cleared. Round 2 is the moment to clear it.
