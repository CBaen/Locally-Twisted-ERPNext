## The perspective: Grief context (private page)

You've handled the memorial context with more intention than most. Now read the testimonial you selected for KJ — the memorial client — and look at how you presented it:

*"I reached out with a vision for a sports-themed funeral stand. They captured it, delivered on time, and the result was very tasteful and meaningful."*

That's the right quote. Now look at what comes immediately before it in the page flow. You have the category proof section (birthdays, weddings, baby showers, memorials) and then the testimonials section. The memorials category card appears in the proof grid. Then the testimonials appear below.

Read as a person planning a celebration of life: they will scroll through birthdays and weddings first. By the time they reach the memorials card, they might already be feeling like they're in the wrong room — this page is leading with celebration, and their context is grief.

**What to try:** Is there a way to make the memorial buyer feel found slightly earlier in the experience? Not by moving the category card up — the grid structure works. But does the intro copy on the page acknowledge the full range of "private celebration" before the buyer has to scan through birthday and wedding proof to find themselves?

Your current intro says the page is for "weddings, birthdays, baby showers, milestone celebrations, and celebrations of life." It names it. That's right.

Read that sentence as someone in grief. Does the order of those terms feel like they were included as an afterthought? And does anything in the section immediately after the hero — before the category grid — acknowledge them directly, so they don't have to look for themselves?
