# Civic & Community — Design Notes (Contestant 7)

## Audience this page serves

City events coordinators, Pride organizers, chamber of commerce staff, county events teams, and public-facing community organizations across the Wasatch Front. These buyers are accountable to a public or membership audience, often procuring through government or nonprofit processes, and need proof that LT can operate at civic scale — not just birthday parties.

## Structural concept: The civic record

This page is designed to feel like a credible vendor summary, not a consumer pitch. The buyer is likely comparing vendors, may require a PO process, and is looking for demonstrated relationships with peer organizations. Every section reinforces "we have done this before, with organizations you recognize, in contexts similar to yours."

## Section-by-section rationale

### 1. Hero
- Image: `seasonal-pride-columns.webp` — rainbow columns at a Pride parade. Establishes civic scale immediately. Parade context says "public event, street-level installation."
- Copy: "Built for the public stage." — The word "public" does double duty: public audience, public event, public accountability. Short and civic-toned.
- Lede names the event types without marketing language: parade routes, city plazas, Pride, chamber, community gatherings.
- CTA passes `?intent=civic` so the contact form can be pre-populated or scoped.

### 2. Proof intro band
- Warm White background: authoritative but not dark. This buyer is reading carefully.
- The key message: civic events are on camera, on a timeline, and in the public record. LT understands that. No frills, no marketing fluff.
- Mentions PO billing and no-middle-agency quoting directly — this is what civic procurement coordinators check.

### 3. Client roster (Stone background)
- All 26 civic/community clients from the approved roster rendered as legible tags.
- Stone background creates a quiet separator between the warm-white intro and the warm-white proof stories below, without stacking two colored bands back-to-back.
- Tag format (bordered chips) makes the list scannable. A coordinator's eye lands on their city or peer organizations immediately.

### 4. Proof stories trio (Warm White)
- Three named examples: SLC Pride (parade), Gallivan Center (venue), Sandy/Herriman (municipal).
- Card format with photo first — the image explains scale before the copy does.
- Client name in brass uppercase, context as Cormorant h3, detail in Lato body copy.
- Photo choices: pride columns (outdoor civic), logo arch (venue entrance), easter arch (community day). Different scales, different contexts — shows range without inventing clients.

### 5. Capability bar (Deep Navy, fullbleed)
- Dark authority band: civic buyers trust structure. Four capability pillars with brass-line icons.
- Parade Route Ready, Utah Rooted, Vendor Credentialed, Delivered and Struck — these are the four things a city coordinator needs to confirm before shortlisting a vendor.
- "Vendor Credentialed" directly addresses the PO/AP billing question in icon form.

### 6. Photo field (visual-field, 4 cells)
- 2×2 on mobile, 4-wide on tablet+.
- No captions, no frame wrappers — images carry the argument.
- Pride columns, logo arch, community arch, festival photo opt — four different civic/community contexts, four different scales.

### 7. Service note (Warm White)
- Audience-specific operational detail: PO process, W-9 availability, parade clearance dimensions, teardown inclusion.
- This is the content a coordinator needs to brief their supervisor. Plain language, no jargon.

### 8. CTA (Ink, fullbleed)
- Darkest possible ground — this is the closing authority statement.
- "Tell us what the city has planned." — Civic-specific, not generic.
- Phone number visible as a fallback for government buyers who prefer a call.

## Anti-defaults avoided

- No sticker badges or clip-art circles
- No light-blue/blush/pastel
- No giant hero (220/250/280px contract enforced)
- No back-to-back colored full-width bands (Stone roster sits between two Warm White sections)
- No lorem ipsum
- No invented clients
- No marketing superlatives ("Utah's #1," "amazing," "stunning")
- No `!important` rules
