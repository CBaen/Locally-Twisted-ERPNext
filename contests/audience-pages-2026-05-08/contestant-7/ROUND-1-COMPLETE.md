# Round 1 Complete — Contestant 7

## Concept

**The Proof is in the Place.** Each of the four audience landing pages is built around the specific buyers, contexts, and proof material that Locally Twisted actually has — not generic event-company copy. Named cities for civic buyers, sector-grouped companies for corporate buyers, named academic calendar moments for school buyers, and category-level proof with privacy commitments for private buyers.

## Signature choices made in Round 1

1. **Buyer-calibrated language** — each page speaks in the professional register of its buyer (municipal procurement, brand accountability, academic calendar, personal milestone).

2. **Roster-as-proof, not roster-as-decoration** — civic clients as scannable tags (peer-city scanning), corporate clients in industry sectors (sector-matching), school clients as an editorial ledger (three named schools, three moments of dignity), private buyers with no named roster (privacy is the explicit promise).

3. **The private page handles memorials directly** — the KJ testimonial about the sports-themed funeral stand is included because it demonstrates exactly what a celebration-of-life buyer needs to see: that LT handled a hard moment with care and taste.

4. **Color discipline** — civic hero uses navy overlay, corporate uses slate overlay, private uses ink overlay. The color register of each hero is calibrated to the buyer's emotional context: civic is authoritative, corporate is precise, school is spirited but controlled, private is intimate and serious.

5. **Privacy as explicit commitment** — "Private by Default" appears as a named capability pillar on the private page, and "private events stay private" appears in both the intro copy and the service note.

## What I'd want to revisit in reflective loops

- The school roster is thin (three named schools). The editorial ledger format gives it dignity, but a Proxy perspective from a graduation coordinator at a school NOT on the list would be useful — does the page still feel credible if your institution isn't named?
- The corporate sector groupings (4 sectors, 4-wide grid) collapse to 2-wide on mobile — worth confirming that the grid still reads well at 375px with the longer sector names.
- The private page uses all three wedding arch photos for the install proof section. That's technically correct (it's what the portfolio has), but it means the birthday and baby shower buyers will only see wedding decor in that section. The category proof cards address this with icons + copy, but the visual proof section skews wedding. Proxy perspective from a birthday parent could surface this.
