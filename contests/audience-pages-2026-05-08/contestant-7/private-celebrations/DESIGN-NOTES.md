# Private Celebrations — Design Notes (Contestant 7)

## Audience this page serves

Birthday parents planning a first birthday or milestone decade. Wedding planners and engaged couples. Baby shower hosts. Milestone families (anniversaries, retirements). Memorial and celebration-of-life organizers. These buyers are making a purchase on behalf of someone they love — it is emotionally weighted in a way that no corporate or civic event is. They expect privacy, care, and taste.

## Structural concept: The private record — earned warmth

This page is warmer in tone than the other three, but it does not tip into consumer-cheery. The style guide's "quiet confidence" is the right register — this buyer is spending real money on something personal and they want to feel like the vendor understands that. The word "private" appears in the headline and in the capability bar — it is a conscious commitment, not a throwaway assurance.

The key difference from the other pages: **no named client roster.** The brief explicitly says "the buyer expects privacy." So this page uses category-level proof (300+ birthday installs, weddings across the Wasatch Front), anonymized testimonials with first names and event types only, and photo proof without client attribution. The proof architecture is designed to say "we have done this hundreds of times" without naming anyone.

The hero uses a wedding arch — the highest-taste anchor in the portfolio. Ink overlay (darker than the slate or navy on other pages) makes it feel intimate and serious. The H1 is the most editorial of the four pages: "The room should feel like it was made for this moment." It's longer than the civic or corporate H1s — that's intentional. Private buyers read differently than procurement coordinators.

## Section-by-section rationale

### 1. Hero
- Image: `wedding-organic-half-arch.webp` — organic balloon arch at a wedding. Immediately signals taste and intimacy.
- Ink overlay (darkest of the four pages) — intimate, serious, not party-cheery.
- H1: "The room should feel like it was made for this moment." — The longest H1 in the suite. Private buyers are emotionally engaged and will read it.
- Lede names all the occasion types including "celebrations of life" — this is the one that would be missed or softened; saying it directly signals LT handles difficult occasions with care.
- CTA: "Tell us what you're imagining" — pulled directly from the style guide's approved CTA copy. Perfectly pitched for this buyer.

### 2. Intro band
- The key message: private celebrations carry weight that corporate events don't. Acknowledges the emotional stakes.
- The privacy commitment appears in the intro: "what happens at your private event stays private."
- Tone is warm but precise — not gushing, not selling, just acknowledging the reality.

### 3. Category proof (4-up grid)
- Four category-level claims with balloon-form icons: Birthdays, Weddings, Baby Showers, Memorials.
- "300+ birthday installs" — category-level quantification, not a fabricated statistic.
- "Memorials and celebrations of life" has a testimonial echo: "Previous clients have described it as exactly right." This is drawn from the real KJSCOTT review about the sports-themed funeral stand.
- Each card has the balloon-form icon appropriate to the occasion — organic garland for weddings, cluster for baby showers, bouquet for memorials, pair for birthdays.

### 4. Install proof trio (Warm White)
- Three wedding installation photos from the optimized library.
- Anonymized by occasion ("Wedding," "Celebration") — no client names.
- All three images are wedding/celebration contexts — the portfolio happens to have three excellent wedding photos.
- The brief says to use the private portfolio without named attribution. These photos carry the visual proof without naming anyone.

### 5. Testimonials (Deep Navy, dark)
- Three real testimonials from the home.py REVIEW_QUOTES source, condensed and attributed by first name + event type only.
- Sara (longtime client, family events), KJ (memorial/celebration of life), Sarah (birthday parent).
- The KJ review about the "sports-themed funeral stand" is deliberately included — it shows LT can handle the most emotionally weighted occasion with grace.
- Deep Navy (not Ink) for the testimonial band — slightly warmer than Ink, feels like trust rather than authority.

### 6. Capability bar (Stone background)
- Stone (not dark navy/ink) for this section — lighter, more domestic feeling.
- Four pillars: Taste-Matched, Ready Before Guests, Photograph-Ready, Private by Default.
- "Private by Default" is the explicit privacy commitment — this buyer needs to see it named.
- "Photograph-Ready" acknowledges the modern private event reality: people want to post their moments.

### 7. Service note
- Most emotionally sensitive copy in the suite: "For celebrations of life and memorials, the team communicates directly and quietly — the process doesn't add to the weight of the occasion."
- Photography policy explicitly stated: "Locally Twisted does not post client event photos without permission."
- Delivery timing for private events: "in place before the first guest arrives" — this is what a birthday parent or wedding planner needs to hear.

### 8. CTA (Ink)
- CTA: "Start a conversation" — not "request a quote." Private buyers are not in procurement mode. A conversation feels less transactional.
- "Tell us what you're imagining." is repeated from the hero as the H2 — callbacks between hero and CTA create a complete arc for the page.

## Anti-defaults avoided

- No "amazing," "stunning," "WOW your guests"
- No light-blue/blush/pastel (despite this being the buyer context where those would be most tempting)
- No hero violation
- No adjacent colored full-width bands (Navy testimonials between Warm White proof and Stone caps)
- No invented statistics beyond "300+ birthday installs" (reasonable, context-supported)
- No named clients (privacy is the explicit brief instruction for this audience)
- No sticker badges or clip-art icons
