# Corporate Events — Design Notes (Contestant 7)

## Audience this page serves

Marketing teams, brand managers, event planners at companies, grand opening coordinators, broadcast and media event staff, financial institution community day organizers, and corporate party planners. These buyers are accountable to brand standards documents, budget approval chains, and often a legal/PR review for any vendor who appears in public with their brand.

## Structural concept: The brand-safe vendor record

This page is a structured vendor brief. It doesn't try to sell with enthusiasm — it presents credentials. The buyer is asking: "Have you done this for companies like ours? Can you match our colors? How does billing work? Will this look good on camera?" Every section is an answer to one of those questions.

The visual approach is intentionally more restrained than the civic or private pages. Slate Blue as the hero dark (instead of Navy or Ink) signals corporate sophistication — it's the color of a conference room, not a party.

## Section-by-section rationale

### 1. Hero
- Image: `corporate-logo-arch.webp` — branded arch at a corporate entrance. Establishes the brand-accountability framing immediately.
- Slate Blue gradient (not ink black) — corporate, controlled, slightly cooler than the civic page.
- H1: "On brand. On time. On record." — three corporate buyer concerns in three beats.
- Lede names the event types (brand activations, grand openings, hospitality events) without marketing language.

### 2. Intro band
- Lead with the color-matching claim — this is the #1 thing corporate buyers check.
- Specific examples: Zions Bank, KSL — named, recognizable Utah corporate brands.
- Billing mechanics mentioned directly in the body copy: AP billing, PO processing, vendor onboarding.
- Quote format named: "materials, labor, delivery, and setup in one line-item format" — this is what a corporate buyer needs to show their finance team.

### 3. Sector proof
- Industry-grouped client cards with brass left-border treatment — structured, scannable.
- Four sectors: Financial Services, Media & Broadcast, Hospitality & Dining, Healthcare.
- Each card has: sector label, client names in Cormorant (editorial), and a one-line operational note.
- This is designed to be read by a buyer who is scanning for "have they worked with companies in my space?" — they find their sector, scan the names.

### 4. Install proof trio
- Three named corporate installs: logo arch (store opening), festival photo op, WSU institutional.
- Different scales and contexts — entry-level recognition that LT can handle institutional color systems, event-scale installs, and broadcast-facing photo moments.
- WSU is technically a university relationship, but university-corporate events are common corporate buyer territory — institutional color matching is the proof point.

### 5. Capability bar (Slate Blue, fullbleed)
- Slate Blue (not navy, not ink) — corporate, professional, controlled.
- Four pillars: Brand Color Match, Repeatable Process, AP-Ready Billing, On-Camera Ready.
- These are the four things a marketing director needs to confirm before adding LT to the approved vendor list.
- "Repeatable Process" addresses the corporate fear of one-time vendors who can't replicate.

### 6. Client roster (Stone)
- Full 30-name corporate client list as legible tags.
- Stone background separates cleanly from the Slate Blue capability bar above and the Warm White service note below.
- Same tag format as civic page — visual consistency across the suite, but each page's content is entirely different.

### 7. Service note
- Named the Pantone/PMS detail explicitly — this is what a corporate brand manager actually cares about.
- "No range pricing" — corporate buyers hate getting a quote range; they need a number.
- Photography clearance mentioned — companies need to know if they can post the photos.

### 8. CTA (Navy)
- Navy (not ink) — professional, but warmer than the Slate Blue capability bar.
- "Tell us what the event calls for" — direct, professional, not pushy.
- Phone number as fallback for buyers who prefer to call the vendor before onboarding.

## Anti-defaults avoided

- No "amazing," "stunning," or generic enthusiasm
- No light-blue/blush
- No clip-art badges
- No adjacent colored full-width bands (Stone roster sits between Slate and Warm White)
- Hero contract enforced (220/250/280px)
- No invented clients or fabricated statistics
- No `!important`
