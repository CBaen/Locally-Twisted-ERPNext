# Schools & Campuses — Design Notes (Contestant 7)

## Audience this page serves

Activity directors, athletic department staff, PTAs, college student life offices, and graduation ceremony coordinators. These buyers are often dealing with tight venue windows, academic calendar constraints, institutional approval processes, and community audiences that include students, parents, faculty, and administrators. Family-appropriate is a hard requirement. School colors are an institutional identity marker — balloons in "close enough" colors are a visible failure.

## Structural concept: The academic calendar specialist

The school roster is short (three named relationships: UofU, WSU, St. Joseph's). The brief says "lean into specific named relationships and graduation/back-to-school context rather than padding with weak items." This page takes that seriously — instead of obscuring the small roster with tag clouds, it presents the three named schools in a structured, editorial proof list that feels deliberate rather than thin. The visual weight comes from the three named moments (graduation, back-to-school, campus events) and specific photos that show ceremony-appropriate work.

The page deliberately avoids "spirit" energy that reads as juvenile. Activity directors and graduation coordinators are professionals. The page speaks to them as professionals — even as it acknowledges the spirit dimension of their work.

## Section-by-section rationale

### 1. Hero
- Image: `school-back-to-school-stage.webp` — large stage balloon backdrop at a school event. Stage context is immediately recognizable to activity directors.
- H1: "Spirit at scale." — Two words that land on both the emotional (school spirit) and operational (civic scale) dimensions. Short, confident.
- Lede names the specific academic calendar moments: graduation, back-to-school, homecoming, campus events.

### 2. Intro band
- The key message is color precision — "University of Utah red and white is not close enough to a generic warm red." This is what a school buyer is actually worried about.
- Weber State's purple is specifically named — this is the kind of detail that makes a coordinator feel seen.
- The second paragraph covers the operational reality: academic calendars, venue windows, family-appropriate.

### 3. School proof (White panel with brass leader lines)
- Three schools, presented with brass rule-dot separators (the editorial treatment).
- Each school gets its name at Cormorant h3 scale, a brass dot leader, and a brief context note.
- This is intentionally NOT a tag cloud — three schools with editorial presence feel more credible than three schools in a tag row that looks like it got lost in a 30-name corporate list.
- The brief says "lean into specific named relationships" — this section does exactly that.

### 4. Moments trio (Warm White)
- Three academic calendar moments: Graduation (garland/arch), Back-to-School (stage), Campus Events (WSU arch).
- Photo first, moment label in brass, h3 context, Lato body detail.
- "Graduation" gets the graduation-specific garland photo, not a generic arch — the images match the moment.
- WSU arch and bouquets card establishes institutional color matching as proof, not just a claim.

### 5. Capability bar (Deep Navy, fullbleed)
- Four pillars: School Color Match, Ceremony Timing, Family Appropriate, Clean Strike.
- "Family Appropriate" directly addresses the administrator's concern — they don't want anything that embarrasses the school in front of parents.
- "Clean Strike" (teardown included) is an operational detail that matters to venue coordinators.

### 6. Photo pair (visual-field, 2 cells)
- Graduation garland and back-to-school stage — the two most common school buyer moments.
- 16:9 cells on mobile, wider on desktop — cinematic proportions that make the installations feel large.
- No captions, no frame wrappers.

### 7. Service note
- Specific operational details: color values in quote request, graduation timing, teardown.
- "No separate line items to argue with administrators" — this is what a buyer needs to get a PO approved.
- School POs explicitly mentioned.

### 8. CTA (Ink)
- "Tell us about the event and your school colors." — Directly names the two things a school buyer needs to provide.
- Ink ground (darkest) for the closing authority statement.

## Design choices specific to this page

The school proof section uses a different visual pattern from the tag-cloud roster on the civic and corporate pages. This was an intentional choice: with only three named schools, a tag cloud would look sparse. The editorial ledger-line format (name + rule + context note) gives the same information more visual dignity and reads as "precision" rather than "shortage."

## Anti-defaults avoided

- No "amazing school spirit!!!" energy
- No light-blue/blush
- No hero violation
- No adjacent colored full-width bands
- No invented schools beyond the three named
- No lorem ipsum
- No clip-art school icons (using brand icon suite)
