# Proxy Loop 1-1 — Contestant 7: "The Proof is in the Place"

## What you've already done well

"When the city puts it on a banner, the balloons have to hold up." is the most quotable single sentence in the entire field. It earns civic accountability in thirteen words without ever using the word "professional." The editorial ledger treatment for the three named schools — giving each one a brass-rule separator and room to breathe — turns a short roster into a strength. That's editorial judgment operating at a high level.

## The perspective: Roster-as-decoration (corporate page)

Your corporate page groups clients by industry sector: Financial Services, Media & Broadcast, Hospitality & Dining, Healthcare. That's among the most sophisticated roster treatments in the field — a marketing director finds their peer industry and sees familiar names. You designed the list to work the way the buyer's eye actually moves.

Now push one step further: are those sector groups carrying proof, or carrying names?

Look at Financial Services: Zions Bank, America First Credit Union, Fidelity, Morgan Stanley. A Utah marketing director at a bank sees that. She recognizes her peers.

But she doesn't know what kind of work LT did for Fidelity. Was it a ribbon cutting? An annual party? A lobby installation? A one-time booth? The sector group tells her LT has worked with financial services clients. It doesn't tell her what that work looked like.

**What to try:** Could one of those sector groups carry a brief context note? Not a full case study — something as small as "grand openings and annual company events" under Financial Services, or "broadcast studio events and premiere screenings" under Media & Broadcast.

That one phrase per sector turns "names I recognize" into "work like mine." The buyer stops scanning for familiar names and starts seeing herself in the proof.

You don't need to do this for every sector. Even two of the four sectors carrying context would shift the roster from a credential wall to a proof grid. Which two sectors does your corporate buyer most need to see contextually?
