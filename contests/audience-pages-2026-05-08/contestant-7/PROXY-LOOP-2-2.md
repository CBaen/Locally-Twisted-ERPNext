# Proxy Loop 2-2 — Contestant 7 (The Proof is in the Place)

## Lens: The proof-vs-promise integrity test

Your corporate page's sector-grouped roster (Media & Broadcast / Financial &
Professional / Restaurants & Hospitality / Healthcare & Corporate / Entertainment &
Events) is a strong proof-density improvement from the Round 1 flat list. A
marketing director scanning the band reads industry recognition, not a name dump.
That's the right move.

But look at the Healthcare & Corporate sector grouping. IHC and Mountain Star
Medical are in there — both real, both credible. The claim those names implicitly
make is: LT works in healthcare environments, which have specific requirements
(latex sensitivity, infection control, sterile-field proximity). The names make that
claim. Does your corporate page prove it anywhere?

There's a real difference between having healthcare clients and having demonstrated
healthcare-environment competence. Other vendors might have served Ancestry or Zions
Bank in a conference room. Serving IHC at a hospital opening requires knowing about
latex-free options, clearance from facilities, and working around patient-zone
restrictions. That specific knowledge — which LT appears to have — is the proof that
healthcare clients need to see before they book.

C4's corporate page has "Latex-free options are available for healthcare and
allergy-sensitive environments" in the services copy. You have the healthcare
client names but not that specific capability statement. The names without the
statement leave an implicit promise that the page hasn't fully backed.

This isn't about adding length. It's about one specific sentence in or near the
Healthcare sector group that closes the gap between client name and client trust.
Something like: "Healthcare and medical clients: latex-free options available."
Five words. Directly under or beside IHC and Mountain Star. Proof at the point of
the claim, not two sections away.

What's working: the memorial test is cleared with the cleanest structural
differentiation in the field — standalone section on Stone background with brass
top border, separate from the category grid which is now three cards rather than
four. The grid label "Every kind of celebration" (not "occasion") is a small
word-level decision that does real work.
