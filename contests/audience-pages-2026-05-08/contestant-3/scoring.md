## Self-Reflection

My concept — anxiety-first architecture — was the most explicitly theorized approach in the field. The field summary confirms it was the only suite that made the persuasion structure testable in advance ("a reviewer should be able to point to the anxiety-naming move on every page without being told where to look"). That is a genuine differentiator at the mechanism level.

Where I held firm with documented reasoning (the civic intro structure, the Loop-2 cycle) the reasoning was sound. The bones were correct from Round 1.

Where I should be honest: the anxiety-first mechanism is the clearest differentiator in my README but requires the copy itself to land it on every page — and my copy, while on-voice, did not produce the single most quotable sentence in any page category. C7 owns the civic quotable sentence. C2 owns the private emotional register sentence. C5 owns the schools H2. C8 owns the corporate three-beat. My suite is coherent and the mechanism is real, but the individual copy peaks are lower than the field's best. That is the honest gap.

The private page's memorial handling improved in Round 2 (intro claim, structural proof) but the anxiety-first architecture, applied to the grief buyer's specific fear ("will this feel appropriate, will they understand what this occasion asks for"), was not as explicitly rendered as it was on the civic or corporate pages. The mechanism was present but the named anxiety move was less visible on private than on the other three pages.

The suite is architecturally disciplined. It is not the leader in proof density (C1, C5, C7 have stronger roster treatments), not the leader in style innovation (C2's border-direction system, C6's four-prose private architecture), and not the leader in quotable copy (C7, C2, C5, C8 each produced the field's best sentence in one category). Its claim is mechanism coherence across all four pages — and that is a real claim, but it is harder to see than a scrolling crawl or a named promise section.
