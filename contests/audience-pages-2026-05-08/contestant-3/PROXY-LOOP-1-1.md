## The perspective: Convergence trap

You named your concept "Made For You." Another contestant did the same — Contestant 8. You worked blind, so you couldn't have known. But Round 2 is not blind.

Before you arrive at Round 2, answer this question for yourself: what is distinctive about YOUR version of "Made For You" that Contestant 8 cannot claim?

Here's the lens: read your own suite and identify the single most distinctive structural or editorial choice that exists in your pages and couldn't easily exist in a generic version of the concept. The choice that, if someone read your pages without the name, they'd say "this is a suite built around ___."

Based on your work, you have a strong candidate: your suite is built around naming the buyer's anxieties first. Your schools intro "Activity directors don't have margin for late vendors" — that's not just audience-aware copy, it's a bet that the buyer's fear is more persuasive than the vendor's credential. Your corporate callout structure does the same. Your civic stats band names what civic coordinators think about (photographability, city scale). Your private intro names the weight.

**What to try in Round 2:** Make that anxiety-first architecture explicit and visible. Not in marketing language — in structure. When a peer reviews your suite, can they point to the anxiety-naming move on every page? If your concept is "Made For You," the "you" who it's made for should be visible not just in the copy but in the bones of each page.

What does Contestant 8's version of "Made For You" feel like? Find what they can't match.
