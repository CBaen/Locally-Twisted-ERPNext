## 2. Field-Wide Patterns

### 📋 Convergences

**Name collision: C3 and C8 both called their concept "Made For You."** They built blind, so this wasn't copying — but the concepts underneath are genuinely different. C3's version is **anxiety-first architecture**: the buyer's fear is named before any credential appears, and the credential exists to answer the fear. C8's version is **named-promise architecture**: each page contains a nameable commitment to the buyer backed by verifiable proof. The distinction is real and the loop docs from both contestants have made it explicit. In Round 2, this name collision becomes a liability only if either contestant fails to differentiate at the mechanism level — both now have the vocabulary to do that.

**KJSCOTT review:** Every contestant who addressed memorial content used this review. It's the only verified social proof for celebration-of-life work in the review set, and it's distinctive ("sports-themed funeral stand... very tasteful and meaningful"). Because everyone reached for it, the differentiation is now in HOW it's deployed — as a card proof line (C1), a panel anchor (C3), an editorial quote in a dedicated section (C5, C8), a testimonial in a full prose block (C6, C7). The review itself no longer differentiates; the structural context around it does.

**Wedding organic half-arch as hero:** The majority chose this image for the private celebrations hero. It's the right call per the brief's available portfolio — but the sameness means Round 2 private pages will look similar at first scroll. Structural and copy differentiation carries more weight than image selection here.

### 🗂️ Structural Clusters

**Client roster treatment:**
- *Flat tag clouds/chips:* C4 (civic and corporate), C5 (civic and corporate)
- *Category-grouped:* C2 (civic by sector type: Cities / Pride / Chambers / Community Venues), C5 after loop (civic by civic type), C6 (civic with labels), C7 (corporate by industry sector)
- *Editorial ledger:* C4 (schools, post-loop), C7 (schools — original round 1 choice)
- *Short-roster-as-feature:* C2 (schools — depth-over-breadth framing), C3 (schools), C7 (schools)

The category-grouped approach is clearly superior to flat tags for large rosters — the Proxy called this out as a proof-density issue. C7 made this call in round 1 for corporate. C5 made it in the loop. C2 made it for civic from the start. Contestants still on flat tags for large rosters should take note.

**Private page architecture:**
- *Category cards + testimonial block:* C1, C3, C4, C8
- *Four full-prose occasion blocks with photo clusters:* C6 (most ambitious architecture)
- *Category proof + dedicated memorial standalone section:* C5
- *Two-column editorial panels with proof anchors:* C2

C6 is the outlier and the most invested. Its four full-prose blocks give the private page the most reading depth, but also the most scrolling weight — the proxy's loop-1-2 note (where's the exhale?) was the only structural concern flagged on any private page.

**Privacy commitment visibility:**
- Named explicitly as a capability pillar: C7 ("Private by Default")
- Named in intro copy + hero lede: C7, C3, C5
- Implied by lack of named clients + anonymized testimonials: C1, C2, C4, C8
- Photography permission policy stated explicitly: C7

C7 is the only contestant who named privacy as a structural commitment rather than a demonstrated behavior.

### 🎙️ Voice Clusters

**Cleanest quiet confidence:** C1 (operational specificity without jargon), C5 ("School colors aren't suggestions" — the best H2 in the field, per proxy), C7 ("When the city puts it on a banner, the balloons have to hold up" — the most quotable single sentence in the field).

**Where voice slipped:** C5 caught its own slip (icon bar body copy dropped from specific to generic after the story block had already proven the specific version — corrected in loop). C3 held firm on its voice choices and documented the reasoning. No contestant's loop introduced voice regressions.

**Copy that earned its place:** C2's celebration-of-life copy ("It is not a department. It is part of the work.") earned the highest specific praise from the Proxy. C8's corporate tagline ("Brand-safe. Repeatable. On your colors.") is tight enough to be a pull quote. C7's civic line is genuinely quotable.

## 4. What This Means for Round 2

### ⚠️ Where the field is converging (avoid copying)

- **Wedding organic half-arch as private hero** — the majority used this image. If you're committed to it, the differentiation has to come from structure and copy, not image.
- **KJSCOTT review deployed somewhere on private** — everyone used it. The question is now WHERE and HOW, not whether.
- **Quiet confidence voice** — almost everyone hit this register correctly. Voice is no longer a differentiator. Structural decisions are.
- **Category-level proof for private** — everyone respected privacy correctly. The differentiation is in proof depth and occasion coverage, not proof existence.

### 🎯 Where the field has open territory

**Memorial depth is the clearest open territory.** C5 has a standalone memorial section. C6 has a full prose memorial block within a four-occasion architecture. C7 claims the grief buyer explicitly in the intro before the category grid begins. Most other contestants have the KJSCOTT review placed well but haven't given the memorial buyer a structural entry point that claims them *before* they have to scan through birthday and wedding proof. The Proxy's "no contestant has fully cleared the memorial test" observation means this is still winnable.

**Civic operational specificity** remains differentiated. C1 added the civic procurement card section in its loop. C6 addressed civic logistics in its README but the Proxy didn't specifically flag it. The civic page that most thoroughly answers the permit-and-documentation reality for a real Utah city events coordinator is still the page that can win that audience.

**School color commitment as a named section** — C8 has this. No one else does it at that structural level. If you're not C8 and your schools page doesn't have a named commitment around color discipline, that's a gap worth closing.

### 🔄 What "no contestant has fully cleared the memorial test" implies

The Proxy's observation means the winning private page will:
1. Acknowledge the grief buyer *early* — before they have to scroll through birthday and wedding content to find themselves
2. Use KJSCOTT's words in a structural position that earns their trust before they scan the category grid
3. Give memorial work a container that feels commensurate with its weight — not a card among cards

The current field is close. C7 added an intro paragraph claiming the grief buyer explicitly. C5 built a standalone section. C6 gave it full prose block parity. None has done all three simultaneously. Round 2 is the moment to do all three.
