# Round 2 Complete — Contestant 8: "Made For You" (Named-Promise Architecture)

## Path taken: B-lean

Named-promise architecture is the mechanism. Round 2 committed harder to it across all
four pages rather than pivoting.

## What changed

### Schools hero
The lede previously buried "in your exact school colors" at the end of a list. The H1
now leads: "Your school colors. Exactly right." — the named promise is the first thing
the activity director reads, priming the Section 4 "School Colors, Respected" block as
reinforcement rather than surprise.

### Private celebrations — memorial sequencing
The memorial section moved from Section 4 (after category grid + photos) to Section 2
(immediately after the hero). The grief buyer is now claimed before any birthday or
wedding content appears. The hero lede was also updated to include "celebrations of life"
explicitly, so the grief buyer is acknowledged at first glance. The structure is now:
Hero → Memorial claim (with KJSCOTT callout) → Categories → Photos → Icons → CTA.

### Civic — "Public-Event Ready" named-promise section added
A new section between the case study and service notes makes three specific commitments
the coordinator can hold LT to: weather-anchored outdoor installs, one-vendor full-service
(delivery + strike on a single invoice), and invoice-ready documentation for city/county
AP. This is the civic page's named promise — not a capability claim, a contract.

### Corporate — sector-grouped client roster
The flat client list is now grouped into five labeled sectors: Media & Broadcast /
Financial & Professional / Restaurants & Hospitality / Healthcare & Corporate /
Entertainment & Events. Each sector is a column with a brass-rule separator. A marketing
director scanning the band now reads industry recognition, not a name dump. CSS added to
the colocated PAGE_CSS block for the sector grid layout.

## What held firm

- Proof-then-promise sequence throughout (not anxiety-first — that's C3's mechanism)
- Schools proof band before the color-match commitment (institutional proof earns the
  named promise; reversing the order makes it a claim, not a contract)
- "Brand-safe. Repeatable. On your colors." on corporate hero — still the tightest copy
  in the suite
- "Not close enough. Exactly right." on schools — held
- "Start a conversation" as the private CTA (softer than "Request a quote" — right
  register for the grief buyer and the milestone buyer alike)

## Suite state

All four pages have at least one named-promise section legible as a structural commitment:
- Civic: "Public-Event Ready" (three promises, three columns)
- Corporate: "Brand-safe. Repeatable. On your colors." (hero tagline) + sector proof
- Schools: "School Colors, Respected" / "Not close enough. Exactly right."
