# Loop 2 Complete — Contestant 8: "Made For You" (Named-Promise Architecture)

## What the loops said

**Loop 2-1 (convergence-survival test):** The civic page's named-promise section landed with
strong proof density and three specific commitments, but the H2 eyebrow "Public-Event Ready"
was a capability label — it described LT's state rather than addressing the coordinator. The
mechanism requires the promise to feel like it's being made TO the buyer, not ABOUT the vendor.
Compare to schools: "Your school colors. Exactly right." That one works because "Your" is in
the first word.

**Loop 2-2 (6-month maintainer):** The named-promise sections across all four pages are
architectural commitments, not decorative copy — but the DESIGN-NOTES didn't say that
explicitly. A future build instance editing "Not close enough. Exactly right." as an aesthetic
refresh would break the mechanism without knowing it. The fix: a single explicit sentence in
each named-promise section making the architecture visible to whoever opens the file next.

## What changed

### Civic HTML — eyebrow and H2 readdressed to the buyer

**Before:**
- Eyebrow: "Public-Event Ready" — capability label, describes LT
- H2: "Three things civic coordinators need. All three, every time." — third person

**After:**
- Eyebrow: "Your event. Our accountability." — addresses the coordinator directly; "Your" first
- H2: "Three things your city needs from a vendor. All three, every time." — "your city" makes
  it the coordinator's event, not a generic civic context

The three commitment columns (weather-anchored, one-vendor full service, invoice-ready
documentation) were already in buyer-facing language and held unchanged.

### DESIGN-NOTES — durability sentences added to all four pages

Each named-promise section now contains an explicit flagged note:

- **Civic** (`lt-civic-promise` section): "This section is architecture, not copy. Do not
  paraphrase or soften these commitments. Changing the language removes the mechanism."
- **Schools** (`lt-school-colors` section): "This section is architecture, not copy. Do not
  paraphrase or soften 'Not close enough. Exactly right.' or the Weber State specificity."
- **Corporate** (Distinctive move): "This tagline is architecture, not copy. Do not paraphrase
  or soften it. A marketing director reads 'Brand-safe. Repeatable. On your colors.' as three
  specific commitments she can hold LT to."
- **Private** (`lt-private-memorial` section): "This section is architecture, not copy. Do not
  soften, abbreviate, or reposition it." — includes note that KJSCOTT is the only verified
  proof for this context and must not be replaced with a generic placeholder.
