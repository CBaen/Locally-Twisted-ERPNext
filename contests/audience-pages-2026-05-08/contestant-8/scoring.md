# Peer Scoring — Contestant 8 (Made For You / Named-Promise Architecture)

Scored on three dimensions, 1–10 each:
1. **Audience fit** — does each page feel made for its specific buyer?
2. **Proof density** — does the real roster + real photo library carry believable weight?
3. **Style discipline** — does the suite obey the style guide while still bringing craft?

---

## Contestant 1 — Audience Authority

- **Audience fit: 9/10.** The operational depth here is genuinely impressive — the civic page's W-9/COI/PO/net-30 vendor documentation cards speak the exact language a Sandy City accounts-payable coordinator uses internally. The corporate scrolling client crawl is a structural signal that says "we serve this many brands" without a word of copy. The private page's Round 2 addition — the slate-blue memorial entry section before the category grid — claims the grief buyer in the first scroll with KJSCOTT's full review in structural position. The schools page names custodial constraints and schedule-tight windows. Every page was clearly written from inside the buyer's operational context. The one thing holding this back from a 10: the civic stats row ("20+ cities served," "Wasatch Front to back range") is slightly generic compared to the specificity everywhere else on that page.
- **Proof density: 9/10.** The 26-client civic grid with category labels is the most complete proof deployment on any civic page. The corporate crawl is distinct — 30 names moving on an ink ground is a different visual claim than a static grid. The private page deploys six real Google reviews including KJSCOTT and the seven-year-relationship review. No invented clients, no inflated stats. Only small gap: the schools page names three schools (correct per brief), but the case story copy is slightly less specific than the civic and corporate proof sections.
- **Style discipline: 8/10.** Hero contract honored. No adjacent full-width colored sections. Cormorant/Lato throughout. The brass bottom-border on schools service cards is a legitimate style guide move. The one concern is the proof stats row on civic — "Wasatch Front to back range" as a proof-cell label reads more like placeholder than editorial copy, slightly off the "quiet confidence" register.
- **Total: 26/30**

## Self-Reflection

My suite (C8, "Made For You / Named-Promise Architecture") brought a genuine mechanism — the named structural commitment backed by verifiable proof — and documented it explicitly enough to survive future build instances. The "School Colors, Respected" section with "Not close enough. Exactly right." is a genuine structural differentiator, and the field summary confirmed it. The corporate tagline "Brand-safe. Repeatable. On your colors." is tight enough to function as a pull quote.

Where I fell short compared to the field's best work:

**Voice.** C7's civic H2 ("When the city puts it on a banner, the balloons have to hold up.") and C2's private editorial ("It is not a department. It is part of the work.") operate at a compressed, quotable level I didn't consistently reach. My strongest copy lines hold up; my supporting copy is more functional than editorial.

**Civic page.** My civic page is proof-dense and technically correct, but the Round 2 eyebrow/H2 revision ("Your event. Our accountability.") improved the address without fully achieving the specificity that C1's vendor-documentation cards brought. The coordinator needed more operational specificity than my named-promise columns provided.

**Proof architecture.** C5's sector-labeled corporate roster (with specific work-type annotations per client) and C7's editorial school ledger are both stronger proof architectures than my versions of those same sections. My roster treatments are correct but not as specifically organized for the buyer's scanning pattern.

**Where I genuinely led.** The named-promise architecture as an explicit structural commitment is the most clearly documented persuasion mechanism in the field. The DESIGN-NOTES durability sentences (preventing future softening of the committed copy) are a contribution no other contestant made in the same explicit form. The schools color-match section is the only standalone named section built around a single, accountable promise in the entire field. These are real contributions, but C7 matched or exceeded the overall execution quality while also bringing the field's best single-page writing.

Honest ranking of the field from what I read: C7 leads. C1, C2, and C5 are closely grouped behind. C3, C4, and I (C8) are the next cluster. C6 brings the most ambitious private-page architecture but pays a scroll-depth cost and doesn't fully solve the early grief-buyer claim.
