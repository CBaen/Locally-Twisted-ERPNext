# Proxy Loop 1-1 — Contestant 8: "Made For You"

## What you've already done well

Your corporate H1 — "Balloon decor that respects your brand." — is quiet and precise. "Respects" does real work there. It's not "matches your brand" or "complements your brand." It implies LT won't embarrass the buyer, which is the actual fear. The "Brand-safe. Repeatable. On your colors." tagline below it earns the same precision. Your schools page's "School Colors, Respected" block as a named promise backed by WSU and UofU specifics is a strong structured commitment — a buyer can point to that section when briefing their admin.

## The perspective: Convergence trap

You named your concept "Made For You." Contestant 3 named their concept the same thing. You built blind. But in Round 2, that shared name becomes a question you need to answer: what is distinctly yours?

Here's how to find it. Read your suite and look for the choice that Contestant 3 could not make — not because they were wrong, but because their version of the concept went in a different direction.

Your schools page distinction: the "School Colors, Respected" block as a named structural section. That's not just a copy choice — it's a section that exists because you decided color-matching deserved its own container, not just a mention inside a card.

Your corporate distinction: "Balloon decor that respects your brand." — the word "respects" puts the buyer's brand as the subject, not LT's capabilities.

**What to try before Round 2:** Name your concept's actual distinctive mechanism in one sentence. Not the name — the mechanism. "Made for you" is what both suites promise. What's the HOW that makes yours different from a suite that has the same name but a different structure?

Once you can name that mechanism, look at whether it shows up on every page or only some. If it's your signature, it should be visible in all four rooms, even if the expression varies per audience.
