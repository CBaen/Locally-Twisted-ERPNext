## My mechanism — named

The proxy asked me to say it in one sentence. Here it is:

**My suite's mechanism is "named-promise architecture" — each page contains at least one section that makes a specific, nameable commitment to this buyer (not a capability statement, a commitment), and backs it with concrete proof.**

The "School Colors, Respected" section is the clearest expression: it doesn't say "we can match your colors." It says *we have*, names two universities, and explains how. That's a named promise backed by receipt.

The same pattern appears elsewhere: the corporate "Brand-safe. Repeatable. On your colors." tagline is three named promises in eight words. The civic "12 Utah cities" count is a quantity-anchored promise that the coordinator can verify. The private memorial section makes the promise of dignity, backed by KJSCOTT's words.

This is what separates my version from a suite that might carry the same concept name. "Made for you" is the feeling. Named-promise architecture is the method.

## Where I hold firm

**On the schools page structure:** The proxy asks whether the school-colors promise could arrive earlier — as the buyer's very first post-hero scroll rather than Section 4. I understand the logic. But I'm holding the current order.

Here's why: the client proof band (Section 2) does something the schools page actually needs before a named promise lands. The proof roster for schools is small — three named institutions. That smallness, displayed at large Cormorant type in a dark authority band, signals scale-appropriate confidence. An activity director scanning the page needs to know LT has done this at institutional level before she'll trust a named promise. If the promise arrives first with no proof behind it yet, it reads as a claim. After the proof band, it reads as a contract.

The sequence is: *We've done this → Here's what the work looks like → Here's the specific commitment.* Reordering to *Here's the commitment → We've done this* would mean the promise is floating when the buyer first sees it.

**On the convergence question:** I'm not pivoting the name. If Round 2 gives me space to differentiate, I'll differentiate via mechanism — "named-promise architecture" is now the vocabulary I'll lean into. The name "Made For You" stays because it's accurate and the mechanism underneath it is distinct enough to survive comparison.

## What I would sharpen in Round 2

One weak point the proxy didn't flag but I'm flagging myself: the schools hero lede is doing double work.

> "Graduation ceremonies, back-to-school kickoffs, homecoming, and campus events — balloon installations in your exact school colors, installed before the first student arrives."

"In your exact school colors" is the most important phrase in that lede — it's the seed of the named promise that will grow into Section 4. But it's buried in a list. Round 2, if I have time, I'd restructure the lede to lead with the color-match commitment rather than end with it. Something closer to: "Your school colors. Graduation, homecoming, back-to-school, and campus events installed before the first student arrives."

That moves the distinctive claim to the front, which primes the buyer to receive the Section 4 promise as a reinforcement rather than a surprise.
