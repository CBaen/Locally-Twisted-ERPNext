# Proxy Loop 2-1 — Contestant 8 (Made For You / Named-Promise Architecture)

## Lens: The convergence-survival test

C3's anxiety-first mechanism and C8's named-promise mechanism are genuinely
different — and both have been executed clearly enough that they can be told apart.
But with the memorial test now cleared by both, the differentiator has moved to
the other three pages.

Here's where C8 is strongest: schools. The H1 "Your school colors. Exactly right."
is the most direct version of a named promise in the field. The activity director
reads it and immediately understands what she's being promised and what she can
hold LT to if the colors are wrong. The "School Colors, Respected" section deepens
that promise with "Not close enough. Exactly right." — the same commitment in a
different register.

Here's where C8 has an opportunity: civic. The "Public-Event Ready" section added
in Round 2 makes three specific commitments (weather-anchored, single-invoice,
AP-ready documentation). Those are named promises. They're good ones. But the
section's H2 — "Public-Event Ready" — is more of a capability label than a promise.
Compare it to the schools H1: "Your school colors. Exactly right." That's a named
promise because it's addressed to the buyer ("Your") and because it closes a
question she was already asking. "Public-Event Ready" doesn't address anyone; it
describes a state.

The question: is there a civic version of "Your school colors. Exactly right."?
Something like "Your city's event. On time. On record." Or shorter. The mechanism
is named-promise — which means the promise should feel like it's being made TO the
coordinator, not about LT's capability. The civic page has strong proof density and
a real structural move in Round 2. Making the named promise's language address the
buyer directly would complete the mechanism across all four pages.

What's working: keeping "Start a conversation" as the private CTA instead of
"Request a quote" is one of the most precise emotional calibration decisions in the
field. The buyer who just read the memorial section isn't ready to "request." She's
ready to "start."
