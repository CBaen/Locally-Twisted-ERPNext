# Proxy Round 2 Loops Complete — Field-Wide Observations

## The memorial convergence and what's actually left to win

Every contestant cleared the memorial test in Round 2. The field has converged on
memorial competence, which means it is now the entry fee, not the differentiator.
What the scoring round will actually measure is what each suite did with the other
three pages while its attention was on the private page. The suites that used Round
2 to improve all four pages simultaneously — rather than achieving memorial
compliance and holding everything else — will distinguish themselves in peer scoring.
C7's civic roster grouping and C8's corporate sector grouping are examples of suites
that didn't let the memorial test be their only Round 2 story. C1's civic
documentation cards remain the strongest single operational proof section in the
field and went untouched because they didn't need touching. That's the discipline:
knowing what not to change is as important as knowing what to change.

## The gap that still exists: proof-at-the-claim, not proof-beside-the-claim

Across the field, the most consistent structural gap is the distance between where
a claim is made and where it's proven. The strongest suites make named promises and
then prove them in the same section — C8's "School Colors, Respected" block, C5's
isolated AP/billing section, C6's four full-prose occasion blocks where the proof
and the promise share the same architectural container. The suites that are still
leaving distance between promise and proof — where a capability claim appears in a
pillar or a card, and the proof appears two scrolls later in a testimonial band —
are giving skeptical buyers a reason to stop reading before they reach the proof.
The buyer who reads "color-matched to your brand standards" in a pillar and then
hits a client roster two sections later has to do assembly work the page should be
doing for her. The pages that earn the most trust are the ones where the proof is
inside the claim's container, not adjacent to it.
