## The perspective: Buyer-in-the-wrong-room (civic vs. corporate)

Put yourself in the chair of a Sandy City events coordinator. She's comparing two vendors. She found LT through a Google search, clicked "Civic & Community," and is reading your civic page. She likes what she sees — 26 orgs, Pride credentials, photos of outdoor work. She's ready to reach out.

Now she accidentally clicks the corporate tab in her browser and lands on your corporate events page. At what moment does she know she's in the wrong room?

Read your corporate hero: *"From brand activations to store openings — balloon decor for corporate and professional events."* That line is clear. She knows immediately.

Read your ink client crawl. She'd recognize Utah Jazz, KSL, KUTV. She'd probably feel out of place.

Good. The rooms feel different.

But now read the corporate service cards. "Brand-Color Matched," "AP/Accounts Payable Invoicing," "Professional Strike." She might actually want some of that language applied to civic events. Are her pages getting the same logistical specificity?

**What to try:** Look back at your civic service cards. You have "outdoor durability," "all-day anchoring," "civic photography," "clean strike." Those are good. But your civic buyer also cares about permits, venue coordination, and whether LT can send documentation to city procurement. You answered that for corporate (AP invoicing, COI available) — does civic get its equivalent? A Sandy City coordinator also submits vendor documentation. That might be the civic room's version of the AP section.

You've built two distinct rooms. Round 2 is the time to make sure each room has its own version of operational proof — not just different aesthetics.
