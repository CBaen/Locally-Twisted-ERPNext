# Schools & Campuses — Design Notes

## Concept signature: "Spirit + Schedule"

The schools page is designed for an activities director at a high school or a
student life coordinator at Weber State. These buyers operate on a tight schedule
— gym is booked until an hour before the event, custodial won't stay late, the
budget comes from a PTA account or a student life fund. The page acknowledges
these real operational constraints and proves LT has done this before at named
schools.

## Page structure rationale

### 1. Hero
- Background: `school-back-to-school-stage.webp` — a large school stage balloon
  installation. Immediately proves school scale and context.
- Slate Blue overlay — school pages should feel authoritative but slightly
  lighter than corporate. Slate is the middle register.
- Single CTA: "Request a School Event Quote." Schools have simpler procurement;
  one clear action.
- Copy: "School spirit, graduation, and campus events — designed for the moment."
  "Designed for the moment" speaks to the ephemeral, schedule-bound nature of
  school events.

### 2. Named school clients
- Warm white band. Three cards with navy top border.
- The client list is short (University of Utah, Weber State, St. Joseph's) —
  so the design FEATURES them instead of hiding them in a grid.
- Each card shows the abbreviation (UofU, WSU, St. Joseph's) as the lead —
  that's how school events coordinators think and talk.
- Note field is specific: "Weberstock festival + campus installations" not
  generic "school events."

### 3. Case story
- White band. Two-column layout.
- Photo: WSU arch and bouquets — the most school-specific image in the portfolio.
- Copy digs into the SPECIFIC operational constraint: "Schools have tight windows."
  This is the line that makes a school activities director nod and stop scrolling.
- Names the specific concern: gym schedule, hallways can't be disrupted, custodial.

### 4. Photo gallery
- Stone band. Four equal-square images.
- All four directly school-context: back-to-school stage, graduation garland,
  WSU arch bouquets, Weberstock photo op.
- Square aspect ratio (1:1) — feels more like a year-book page, appropriate
  for school context.

### 5. Services
- Near-white band. Four cards with brass bottom-border accent (reversed from
  civic's top-border — small differentiator across the suite).
- "School Color Arches" vs "Parade & Entrance Arches" — same service, different
  name for the specific buyer.
- "Graduation Ceremony Decor" is explicit — this is the most searched school
  balloon term.

### 6. Buyer notes
- Slate Blue dark band — creates visual weight in the lower half of the page.
- Four practical notes: color matching (school colors), schedule-tight installs,
  clean strike, PTA/activity budget invoicing.
- These are presented in near-transparent dark cards — warm against the slate.

### 7. CTA
- Deep Navy fullbleed. Crimson button.
- Copy: "Tell us what you need." — direct, practical, not emotional like the
  private celebrations page.

## Photo choices
- Hero: `school-back-to-school-stage.webp`
- Clients: (no photos — card design with text proof)
- Story: `corporate-wsu-arch-bouquets.webp` — the most school-specific image available
- Gallery: Back-to-school stage, graduation garland, WSU arch, Weberstock photo op

## Audience-specific moves
- "Gym scheduled until an hour before" copy — specific operational reality
  that only a school coordinator would recognize and trust
- PTA budget in buyer notes — real payment mechanism, real buyer concern
- Abbreviations (UofU, WSU) in client cards — school-buyer shorthand
- "Clean strike" in buyer notes phrased as "no cleanup left for your custodial
  staff" — the exact way school buyers think about this

## Container discipline
- Hero: fullbleed
- Named clients: band (warm white)
- Story: band (white) — different surface from warm white to create subtle
  color variation
- Gallery: visual-field (stone)
- Services: band (near-white)
- Buyer notes: fullbleed (slate — treated as full authority band)
- CTA: fullbleed (navy)

The slate buyer-notes band between services and CTA creates a visual
"authority close" — the page ends on dark, informed, professional confidence.
