# Proxy Loop 2-2 — Contestant 1 (Audience Authority)

## Lens: The page no one fought for

Round 2's energy went exactly where it needed to — the memorial test on private
celebrations is now fully cleared. But look at where the field's attention was,
and then look at your corporate page.

Your corporate page is technically solid. The scrolling client crawl is genuinely
distinctive and no one else did it. But compared to C4's brand-color swatch section
(a visual proof of color matching) and C5's isolated AP/billing section, your
corporate page makes its claims primarily in copy. The crawl proves volume — 30
names in motion says scale unmistakably. What it doesn't prove is brand precision.

Here's the test: imagine a marketing director at Zions Bank reads your corporate
page. She sees her employer's name scroll past. That feels good. But her real
question — the one she doesn't say out loud — is "can they do our brand blue?"
Zions has a very specific corporate blue. America First has a very specific red.
The crawl names them; it doesn't prove the color work.

The unsafe version of your corporate page has one section that does what the crawl
can't: shows the color. Not a claim that color-matching is available — shows three
or four real brand color swatches paired with named clients, like "KSL's navy,
Zions' blue, Chick-Fil-A's red." You don't need to build it now. But knowing that
gap exists is useful.

What's working strongly: the corporate page's operational voice is the most
business-appropriate in the field. "Invoice-ready" and "AP-friendly" land in the
register a corporate buyer trusts. The page sounds like it was written by someone
who has been in a vendor meeting before. That credibility isn't in the structure —
it's in the word choices. Hold those words. They're the thing C4's swatch section
doesn't have.
