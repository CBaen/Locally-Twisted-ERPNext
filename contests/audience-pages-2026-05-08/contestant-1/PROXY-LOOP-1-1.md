## The perspective: Grief context (private page)

Read your private celebrations page as someone planning a celebration of life for a parent who died unexpectedly. Not a milestone birthday. Not a wedding. A funeral stand.

They're on your page because they Googled "balloons for a memorial service" at midnight and they're exhausted and they don't know if this is even the right vendor.

Now read your celebration type cards again.

"Memorials & Celebrations of Life: Tasteful, meaningful arrangements for celebration-of-life services. Sports themes, favorite colors, and personal tributes handled with care. *Proof: Verified by customer reviews*"

The proof line at the bottom of that card — "Verified by customer reviews" — reads like a Yelp disclaimer. This is the only card in your suite where the proof line sounds like fine print rather than human warmth.

The KJSCOTT review you placed in your testimonials is actually one of the most powerful pieces of copy on this entire page: *"I needed a sports themed funeral stand. I told them what I needed, they captured my vision, delivered on time, very reasonable, and had many complements. Very tasteful and meaningful."* That review is doing the work. The card note above it is not.

**What to try:** Move the KJSCOTT review closer to the memorial card itself — or let that review's voice write the card copy instead of the generic "handled with care" construction. The real review says "they captured my vision." That phrase is the proof. The card copy above it is just a label.

The person planning a celebration of life doesn't need to be told the work is "meaningful." They need to feel that you've done this before and you know what that means.
