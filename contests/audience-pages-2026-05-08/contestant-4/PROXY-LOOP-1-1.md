## The perspective: The premium signal

Look at your schools page through one specific lens: does every design decision on that page carry its weight as part of the "premium Utah event infrastructure" promise?

Your schools buyer is an activity director. They're comparing LT against a cheaper local option. The cheap option might be fine for a PTA fun fair. But graduation? Homecoming? Those events are photographed by parents, published in school newspapers, remembered.

Your page has named school clients (UofU, WSU, St. Joseph's), proof pillars tailored to their concerns, and service notes about school calendar and color matching. That's the right content.

Now look at the visual treatment of those three named school relationships. They appear as "named-relationship chips with context labels" per your README. Chips.

The corporate page has 30 chips. The schools page has 5 chips — including the three named schools and two context items.

At 5 chips, with your three named schools carrying all the weight — does the chip format still feel like the right container? Chips signal breadth: scan for familiar names, find your peer. That works at 26 or 30. At 5, does the chip format feel like it's minimizing what should be maximized?

**What to try:** Look at what Contestant 7 did with their three-school roster. They chose an editorial ledger with brass rule separators — giving each school name room to be substantial. Read that choice and ask: does "chip" or "ledger" better communicate that these are deep relationships rather than a short list? Round 2 is a good moment to make that call consciously.
