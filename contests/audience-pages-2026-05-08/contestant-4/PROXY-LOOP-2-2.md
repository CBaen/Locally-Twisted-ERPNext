# Proxy Loop 2-2 — Contestant 4 (One System, Four Buyers)

## Lens: The voice-on-the-edge test

Find the line in your suite closest to slipping into vendor-speak or sentimental.
For your corporate page, the candidate is in the services section: "Corporate events
live or die by precision: the arch goes up before the doors open, the colors match
the brand kit, and the invoice is clean enough for AP."

Read that sentence carefully. It's close to being excellent — and close to being
a sales brochure. "Live or die by precision" is slightly elevated for the register.
A corporate marketing director reading this is calm, not dramatic. She doesn't
experience her event as life-or-death. She experiences it as a checklist: arch up,
colors right, invoice clean. You named her checklist correctly. The "live or die"
framing is yours, not hers.

The line without the elevation: "Corporate events have three requirements: the arch
is up before the doors open, the colors match the brand kit, and the invoice is
clean for AP." That version is harder. Less writerly. More the marketing director's
interior monologue than the vendor's pitch. Whether the current version or the
stripped version is right is a judgment call — but it's worth knowing which side
of the line you're on.

Now look at the private page's invitation-centered Section 4 ("If you're here for
a celebration of life, you're in the right place"). This is on the right side of
the emotional register — direct address without sentimental decoration. That's the
correct calibration for the grief buyer. The corporate page's "live or die" phrase
is the one place in the suite where the company's voice comes slightly ahead of
the buyer's.

What's working: the memorial test is fully cleared with architectural rigor —
Section 2 (early claim + KJSCOTT proof) and Section 4 (named invitation + standalone
container) serve different functions and that difference is documented. The suite
is the most formally structured of the eight. That's a real strength going into
peer scoring.
