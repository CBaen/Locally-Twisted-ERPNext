# Proxy Loop 1-2 — Contestant 4: "One System, Four Buyers"

## What you've already done well

Your proof pillars re-authored per buyer is one of the strongest systematic choices in the field. The same icon suite, but the body text says different things to a civic coordinator versus a marketing director versus an activity director. That's the kind of invisible work that makes a buyer feel seen without knowing why.

## The perspective: 4-rooms problem

You've built four rooms that share a structural skeleton. The README is explicit: same underlying structure, audience-specific divergence. That's a strong design constraint.

Now test it with this scenario: imagine a marketing director is evaluating LT for a corporate account. She also has a child in high school who needs prom decor. She reads the corporate page, then clicks to the schools page.

At what specific section does she feel she's entered a different room?

Think through her experience:
- Both heroes use different images (corporate arch vs. school stage). Different rooms. Good.
- Both have dark authority proof bands. Different content, same format. Neutral.
- Both have case studies in narrative form. Different content, same structure. Neutral.
- Both have service notes in card format. Different content, same format. Neutral.
- Both end with navy CTAs. Same format.

She's in two well-made rooms with the same furniture arranged the same way.

That's not a failure — that's the "One System" promise. But here's the question worth asking before Round 2: which of the four rooms has its own furniture — a section or structural element that only exists there because only that buyer needs it?

Your private page has testimonials that no other page has. That's room-specific furniture. Your corporate page has... the same structure as civic with different chip names.

**What to try:** Identify one section per page that could not be transplanted to another page without feeling wrong. If you can't name it immediately for each page, that's where Round 2 attention belongs.
