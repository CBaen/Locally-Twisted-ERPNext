# Schools & Campuses — Design Notes

## Audience
Activity directors, athletic department coordinators, PTAs, college student life offices, graduation organizers.

## Buyer posture
School buyers operate under unique constraints: limited budgets, fixed event calendars, custodial schedules, and an obligation to be appropriate for all ages and family contexts. They are also proud of their school colors — color precision matters to them emotionally, not just professionally.

The brief honestly notes that the school roster is short (UofU, WSU, St. Joseph's, plus community crossovers). Rather than hiding this, the page turns it into a strength: "The school roster is intentionally short — Locally Twisted builds deep relationships with the institutions it serves, not a long shallow list."

## Concept: "In your colors. On your schedule."
Colors + schedule. These are the two things an activity director worries about when hiring any vendor. Colors because the principal and the student body will notice if it's "kind of" the school color. Schedule because the custodians need the gym back by 4pm.

## Section decisions

### Hero
- Image: Back-to-school stage — the most directly school-specific image in the portfolio. A gym-scale balloon stage installation. No ambiguity about audience.
- H1: "In your colors. On your schedule." — The two things. Concise.
- Lede: Arches, stage decor, graduation garlands — specific event types this buyer is planning.

### Client proof
- Small roster presented honestly. A flex-wrap pill list rather than a full column table — appropriate for the size.
- Each pill shows the school name and type (University, High School, Education / Community).
- **Per pill specifics added after Proxy Loop 1-2:** Each pill now carries one specific detail about the relationship — what kind of event, what colors, whether they're a returning client. The names were doing name-level work; they now do relationship-level work. The explanatory note below still reads correctly as a framing statement, but it's no longer carrying an argument the names couldn't make on their own. A Sandy City activity director reading WSU's pill sees "spirit event arch and bouquet cluster, purple and white, photographed for university event coverage" — that's a credential, not a list entry.
- The copy below the list turns the short roster into a trust signal: "multiple events per year, color-matched builds, a coordinator who already has our number saved." This is honest and specific.
- Contrast with the corporate page's 30+ client table — the school page leans into depth over breadth.

### Proof stories (stone background)
- Three stories: WSU spirit events (color-matched university work), back-to-school stage (assembly scale), graduation garlands (the ceremonial moment).
- Each story has a context label (what TYPE of event) + client attribution + headline + body.
- The graduation story specifically names the tension: "The schedule is tight and the photos last forever — Locally Twisted builds for both." This is the graduation coordinator's actual anxiety.

### Trust pillars (navy band)
- Heading "What activity directors and athletic coordinators count on" — names the actual job titles.
- Four pillars: School Colors. Exactly. / On Schedule for School Logistics / Family-Friendly and Safe / Budget-Aware Builds.
- "Budget-Aware Builds" is the honest one: "School budgets are real. Tell us the ceiling and we'll design to it." This builds more trust than pretending pricing isn't a constraint.

### Event type matrix (warm white)
- 8 event types in 2×4 grid (even, no orphans at any breakpoint): Graduation Ceremonies, Back-to-School Assemblies, Homecoming, Pep Rallies, Athletic Events, Senior Night, Dance & Prom Decor, Campus Grand Openings.
- Chips not cards — lighter visual weight than the corporate service cards. Appropriate for a more casual/spirited audience.
- Answers "what can you do for us?" with a scannable list.

### Service notes (sandstone background)
- Sandstone is distinct from stone and warm white — separates this section without creating a third dark panel.
- Bottom-brass-border card treatment, different from the left-brass (civic) and top-brass (corporate) treatments. Each page has a slightly different card treatment that still uses the same brass accent logic.

### Photo gallery (warm white)
- All four images are clearly school/campus work: back-to-school stage, grad garland, WSU arch, campus-appropriate arch.
- Warm white after sandstone is fine (not adjacent same-color full-width sections, because sandstone ≠ warm white and they are different backgrounds).

### Closing CTA (navy)
- Navy matches the trust pillar band, but they are separated by three sections (event matrix, service notes, gallery), so this doesn't violate the "no adjacent colored sections" rule.
- Copy addresses the coordinator's three-item checklist: event, school colors, venue.
- Promises three specific things: deliver on schedule, install before first student arrives, clear before custodians need the space.

## Voice decisions
- "In your colors. On your schedule." — two-beat, mirrors how activity directors think.
- "Exactly." — the period after "School Colors. Exactly." is deliberate. It closes the question before it's asked.
- "The school roster is intentionally short" — honest reframing of a potential weakness.
- "A coordinator who already has our number saved" — implies repeat business, trust, familiarity.
- "Before the first student arrives" — specific, operational, shows Locally Twisted knows school logistics.
- "Budget-Aware Builds" and "Tell us the ceiling" — acknowledges school financial reality without apologizing for it.
