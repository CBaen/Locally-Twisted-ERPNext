## What you've already done well

The card-border variation per audience (left brass for civic, top for corporate, bottom for schools, full surround for private) is one of the most quietly sophisticated micro-decisions in the field. Same system. Different posture. Nobody will consciously notice it but every buyer will feel slightly more seen. The "It is not a department. It is part of the work" line for celebration-of-life is the most human sentence in your suite.

## The perspective: Proof-density on private (no named-client roster)

You made the right call respecting privacy on the private page — brief says to, and it's the correct instinct. Now read that page asking one question: what carried the proof?

You chose four Google reviews (first-name attribution), category-level claims (300+ birthday installs, weddings across the Wasatch Front), and the four celebration-type panels. That's a solid proof structure.

But look at the celebration-type panels. Each panel has an image, a headline, and body copy. The body copy is editorial and feeling-forward: *"The party that felt like you planned it for a year."* / *"Elegant enough for the photographs. Personal enough for the couple."*

Those headlines are beautiful interior-monologue copy. But they are not proof. They are claims the buyer brings to the page — you're feeding them their own hope back.

The real proof is buried in panel stats ("300+ birthday installs") and photo captions. The buyer who's skeptical doesn't know whether LT does 2 private events a year or 200.

**What to try:** Read your panels through the eyes of a first-time buyer who has never hired a balloon company. They love the sentiment. But do they see enough evidence of volume, of range, of others-who-felt-this-way? The testimonial section below the panels does some of this work. Ask whether any of that social proof should migrate closer to the panels themselves — so the moment of "this sounds beautiful" is also the moment of "and here's proof they've done it."
