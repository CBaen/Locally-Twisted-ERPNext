# Corporate Events — Design Notes

## Audience
Marketing teams, brand activations leads, store opening coordinators, broadcaster events teams, bank/credit-union community day planners, corporate party organizers.

## Buyer posture
Corporate buyers are operating inside systems — they have load-in windows, brand standards, AP processes, and a boss who will ask whether the vendor was professional. They are comparing Locally Twisted to other vendors on: (1) brand compliance (will the balloons actually be our colors?), (2) logistics fit (can they work inside our schedule?), and (3) professional documentation (can I pay them through AP and get an itemized receipt?).

The page needs to establish Locally Twisted as the vendor that already knows how this works — not a birthday balloon company that does "corporate too."

## Concept: "On brand. On schedule. On record."
Three words that name the three things corporate buyers need. On brand (color matching). On schedule (coordinated logistics). On record (invoiced, itemized, AP-ready). This triple structure gives the buyer an immediate mental checklist to measure the vendor against.

## Section decisions

### Hero
- Image: Corporate logo arch — the clearest evidence of brand-matched professional work in the portfolio. Immediately signals "we do real corporate installs."
- Eyebrow: "Corporate Events · Brand Activations · Utah" — industry language, not event-y language.
- H1: "On brand. On schedule. On record." — three concise corporate-language anchors.
- Lede: Grand openings, corporate parties, broadcaster events, brand activations — the actual events this buyer is planning.
- CTA: "Request a Corporate Quote" — matches the budget-approval framing a corporate buyer uses.

### Client proof table
- Five industry column groups: Food & Hospitality, Media & Entertainment, Finance & Professional, Technology & Healthcare, Events & Automotive.
- 5-column on desktop — the brief's 30+ corporate clients distribute cleanly across 5 vertical markets. Corporate buyers recognize their industry in the list.
- Brass-ruled category headers.
- This is the "you've worked in our sector before" proof moment.

### Proof stories (stone background)
- Stone background separates from warm white without being a third dark panel.
- Three stories: WeberStock (festival-scale branded moment), Corporate Brand Entrances (generic but honest — color matching and reliability), IHC/Mountain Star (institutional/healthcare context — the buyer who is most worried about "is this appropriate for us").
- Each story has real client attribution, honest scale language.

### Trust pillars (slate band)
- Slate Blue rather than full Navy — distinct from the CTA navy, less severe than ink.
- Four pillars named from the buyer's mental model: Color Matched to Brand Standards, Invoiced Through Accounts Payable, Scalable for Any Venue, Insured and Professional.
- COI mention is prominent — corporate buyers will ask.

### Service notes (warm white)
- Heading "Designed for corporate logistics" — names the system they operate inside.
- Lede explicitly names the constraint types: load-in windows, color systems, ops coordinators.
- Four cards with brass-top border — cleaner geometric treatment than left-border for corporate aesthetic.

### Photo strip (stone — visual-field)
- Stone background is consistent with the proof stories above it — but a warm-white band separates them (services). This is the sequence: stone stories → white services → stone photos → navy process bar → ink CTA.
- Alternating stone/warm-white breaks up the rhythm without stacking adjacent colored bands.

### Process bar (navy — raw-band)
- Thin navy band, horizontal step-by-step in Lato. Quote → Color Match → Install → Strike → Invoice.
- Named explicitly because corporate buyers want to know "what is the process?" before they call.
- Compact. Reads fast. Does not need a section heading.

### Closing CTA (ink — fullbleed)
- Ink is darker than slate, distinct from the navy process bar.
- Copy addresses the coordinator's briefing checklist: venue, color system, load-in window.
- Single berry button.

## Voice decisions
- "On brand. On schedule. On record." — corporate triptych. Direct, non-decorative.
- "Invoiced to AP" — the acronym corporate buyers actually use. Not "easy billing."
- "COI available on request" — states it as matter-of-fact, not as a selling point.
- "Load-in window" — the term a corporate events coordinator uses, not "delivery time."
- "Your color system" — implies brand standards exist and will be respected.
- "Accounts payable directly" — closes the corporate billing loop.
