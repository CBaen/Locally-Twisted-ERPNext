## The perspective: Schools roster honesty

You named the feature explicitly. Now sit with the explanatory note itself and ask whether it's doing the work you think it's doing.

Your note reads (per design notes): presented as "deep relationships, not a long shallow list." Look at your copy — you have three named schools (UofU, WSU, St. Joseph's) presented as pills, and then the note below them.

Here's the question: did you let the smallness be a feature through the specifics you gave each school, or does the note have to carry that argument because the names themselves don't say enough?

In other words: when a Sandy City high school activity director reads your schools page, do the three names feel like relationships or like a list that needs an apology?

**What to try:** Look at each named school and ask how much work it's doing on its own. Does each school name come with a specific detail — what kind of event, what the installation looked like, what the relationship has been? Or are they three names with a note that explains why there are only three?

The difference between a short roster that's a feature and a short roster that's a weakness is whether each entry carries enough weight to stand without the explanatory note. Your instinct was right. The question is whether you gave each name enough to say for itself.
