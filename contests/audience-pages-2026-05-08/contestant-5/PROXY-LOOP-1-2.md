## The perspective: Roster-as-decoration (civic page)

Your civic client proof band is a dark authority band with named clients in a grid. It's early in the page (section 2), which signals "credentials before pitch" — exactly the proof-first instinct your concept names.

Now read those clients the way a Sandy City events coordinator reads them. She's scanning for peer organizations.

Sandy City is on your list. Herriman City. West Point. She sees her peers. Good.

But is the list doing the most it can do? The clients appear as names. Some pages in the field added category labels (Pride orgs vs. city governments vs. chambers) to make the list more scannable. Some added brief context (what kind of events). Yours, per your structural grammar, is a "disciplined grid" — which suggests names without additional framing.

**What to try:** Read the civic client list and ask: does a new visitor understand what kind of work LT did for each client? Or does the list communicate that LT has done *something* for each of those names?

There's a difference between a list that says "we have worked with all of these" and a list that says "we have done this specific kind of work for organizations like yours." The first is a credential wall. The second is proof calibrated to your buyer's world.

You don't have to label every client. But could the organization of the list — by civic type, by geography, by event scale — do work that the names alone aren't doing? That's the round 2 question.
