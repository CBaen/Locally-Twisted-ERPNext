# Design Notes — Corporate Events

## Audience

Marketing directors, event managers, AP coordinators, brand activation leads, store opening project managers, broadcaster event coordinators, and bank/credit union community relations teams. Buyer posture: brand-safe, on-color, professional execution, billable through AP.

## Page intent

This buyer is evaluating vendors, not discovering balloon decor. They know what they want (an arch, a backdrop, columns for a ribbon cutting) and they're deciding whether LT is professional enough to trust with a branded event. The page needs to demonstrate:

1. We've done work with companies you recognize in sectors you trust
2. We understand brand standards and corporate execution
3. We work within your procurement process (AP invoicing, documentation)

The tone is more restrained than the civic page — this is the most conservative of the four audiences.

## Section decisions

### 1. Hero
Background: `corporate-logo-arch.webp` — the strongest corporate credential in the photo library. A brand arch at a professional event entrance, clearly corporate context. Deep Navy/Slate Blue gradient (Navy is heavier here than civic's pure Ink, because corporate buyers respond to authoritative corporate blue). Eyebrow lists the use cases specifically: "Brand activations · Store openings · Corporate events." H1 contains the key word "professional."

### 2. Client proof band (Slate Blue)
30 named corporate clients, displayed with sector labels (Banking & Finance, Broadcast Media, Healthcare, Technology, etc.). Slate Blue rather than Ink — darker than light surfaces but slightly less severe than Ink. This is the authoritative brand secondary. The sector labels do a lot of work: a bank's marketing director scanning this page will see "Banking & Finance" with Zions Bank, America First CU, Fidelity, and Morgan Stanley in the same row and feel immediately understood.

### 3. Photo proof
Four photos from both libraries:
- `corporate-logo-arch.webp` — brand entrance arch
- `corporate-weberstock-photo-opt.webp` — festival photo backdrop
- `corporate-wsu-arch-bouquets.webp` — partnership event with arch and bouquets
- `Mock up IHC.png` — Intermountain Healthcare mockup, demonstrates healthcare sector credibility

### 4. Story block (Stone)
Three paragraphs, editorial register. First paragraph names the specific stakes of corporate events: PR photos, social posts, marketing director approval, brand standards. Second paragraph calls out specific named clients and what those relationships required (conservative, brand-precise, multi-day durability). Third paragraph is logistics: quote process, documentation.

### 5. Process note (Warm White, 4-step)
Corporate buyers want to know the process before they invest time in a quote. Four numbered steps: Quote → Design approval → Day-of install → Strike. The numbered style is editorial (large Cormorant Garamond numerals, brass-tinted, semi-transparent) rather than a bulleted list — premium but structured.

### 6. AP/billing note (Warm White, thin Stone rule separator)
This section is a distinct move for the corporate page and doesn't appear on other pages. AP/procurement is a real objection for large organizations. Three paragraphs: formal invoice/documentation availability, repeat client program, and how to begin. The Stone border-top separates this from the process section without introducing a new background color (which would create the adjacent-colored-band problem).

### 7. Icon bar (Navy)
Four icons: corporate-entrance, trusted-partner, professional, delivery-install. All relevant to the corporate buyer's concerns. Navy background separates from Warm White above (valid by style guide pattern).

### 8. CTA (Ink)
"Ready to discuss your event?" — professional framing. Promise of one-business-day quote response is the key conversion detail for corporate buyers who need to manage internal timelines.

## Color pattern
Slate Blue (proof band) → Warm White (photos) → Stone (story) → Warm White (process + billing) → Navy (icon bar) → Ink (CTA)
No two adjacent colored full-width bands. ✓

## Distinct corporate-only elements
- Sector labels in the client grid (other pages use categories, but corporate needs sector specificity)
- 4-step numbered process section (corporate buyers want process clarity)
- AP/billing note section (unique to this page — no other audience cares about this)
- "One business day" quote response promise in CTA copy
