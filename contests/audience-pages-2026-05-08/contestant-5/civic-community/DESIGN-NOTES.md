# Design Notes — Civic & Community

## Audience

City event coordinators, Pride organizers, chambers of commerce, county events teams, and public-facing community organizations. Buyer posture: public-facing, civic-scale, photographable work. Comparing two vendors and looking for credibility signals, not brand storytelling.

## Page intent

This buyer doesn't need to be persuaded that balloon decor is a good idea — they're already buying it. They need to know Locally Twisted has done real work with real public organizations at real public scale. The page front-loads that proof aggressively and spends very little time on soft brand language.

## Section decisions

### 1. Hero
Background: `seasonal-pride-columns.webp` — Pride columns photographed outdoors, immediately signals civic/community context. Heavy dark gradient on the left so H1 reads clearly on mobile. Eyebrow names the buyer's world ("Utah public events & civic celebrations"). H1 is declarative and specific, not a slogan. No CTA in the hero — the buyer should see the client roster first.

### 2. Client proof band (dark, Ink)
All 26 named civic clients from the brief roster, displayed in a grid with their category (Municipal, Pride & Equality, County & Regional, etc.). Dark Ink background signals authority. Brass labels for category metadata. This section exists to answer "have you done this before?" in three seconds. Grid deliberately dense — this is credential documentation, not marketing.

### 3. Photo proof
Four real installed civic/community photos from both libraries:
- `seasonal-pride-columns.webp` (optimized portfolio, outdoor Pride install)
- Pride/`20_ progress flag arch.png` (Odoo source, parade arch)
- Pride/`Iheart media pride float.png` (Odoo source, civic broadcast client)
- Parades/`Standard arch for parade.png` (Odoo source, community parade)

Photos without client name captions — caption describes install type and context only, which is appropriate for public events where the install itself is the proof.

### 4. Story block (Stone background)
Editorial paragraph that names the unique pressure of civic work: public photography, social sharing, civic newsletters, weather. Not puffery — it's a description of reality that demonstrates the company understands the context. Mentions specific deliverables (delivery, install, teardown included) so the coordinator knows what they're getting.

### 5. Seasonal note (Warm White)
Utah's civic event calendar is year-round. This section tells the coordinator that LT serves every season — useful for organizations that plan far ahead and don't want to start a new vendor relationship mid-year. Also includes a lead-time notice ("popular dates fill early") which is a practical service message, not a pressure tactic.

### 6. Service note (Warm White, no background change — avoids adjacent colored band problem)
Six service cards covering what civic coordinators actually need to know before submitting a quote request: site visit, delivery/install/teardown, outdoor durability, custom color, multi-location, and vendor documentation (insurance certificates for permits). These are the objections and questions this buyer will have before they contact anyone. Answering them on the page reduces friction.

### 7. Icon proof bar (Navy)
Four icons from the brand SVG suite: utah-rooted, civic-parade, professional, trusted-partner. Each with a label and a one-sentence note specific to civic buyers. Navy background separates visually from the Warm White service section above without stacking two colored full-width bands (the service section is Warm White, this is Navy — the contrast is intentional and appropriate per style guide pattern: Warm White content → dark authority band).

### 8. CTA (Ink)
Short invitation copy, not pressure. Contact link with `?intent=civic` query param so the form can be pre-scoped. Button text "Request a civic quote" is specific to the buyer context — not a generic "Contact Us."

## Color decisions
- Dark sections: Ink (#0A0A0B) and Navy (#0E2240) — alternated, never adjacent
- Light sections: Warm White (#FAF7F2) and Stone (#E7E5E1)
- Pattern: Warm White → Ink (proof band) → Warm White (photos) → Stone (story) → Warm White (seasonal + service) → Navy (icon bar) → Ink (CTA)
- No two adjacent colored full-width sections

## Typography
- H1: Cormorant Garamond, Warm White on dark overlay
- H2s: Cormorant Garamond, Ink on light, Warm White on dark
- Body: Lato, Soft Gray
- Labels/eyebrows: Lato 700, Brass or Soft Gray, letter-spaced, uppercase

## Photo choices rationale
Pride work is the most visible and politically meaningful civic work LT does — showing it first signals that LT is comfortable with high-visibility public events. The parade arch and float photos confirm scale and outdoor capability. All four photos are from real installed work.
