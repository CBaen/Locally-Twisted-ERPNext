## What you've already done well

"School colors aren't suggestions." is the best H2 in the field. Full stop. It earns its confidence without explaining itself, and it names the real fear (approximate color matching) in four words. The corporate AP process callout as a distinct dedicated section — not buried in a pillar — shows structural awareness of what that buyer actually needs to see before they shortlist you.

## The perspective: Voice slip (schools page)

Your schools page has some of the sharpest copy in your suite. And it also has a moment where the voice steps slightly out of character — not loudly, but enough to notice.

Read this passage from your story block:

*"A graduation garland has to match the school's colors — not close, not approximately. Red that reads maroon in photos or blue that drifts toward teal breaks the visual discipline that student life offices and parents expect."*

That's excellent. Specific, earned, quiet authority.

Now read this from the icon proof bar:

*"School Spirit Ready: Color-matched installs for school athletics, graduation, and campus events"*

That's a label. It's not wrong — but after the specificity of the story block, it flattens. "Color-matched installs" is the generic description; your story block above it proved the specific version of that claim.

**What to try:** Read each icon proof bar across your four pages and ask: does the body copy under each icon earn its icon label through specificity, or does it summarize back to generic? The icon bars are the last compressed proof before the CTA. They should be the most distilled version of the specific thing — not a restatement.

The bar that says "School Spirit Ready" with generic body copy is a wasted opportunity to put one final specific claim in front of the activity director right before they decide to click your CTA. What's the most specific, most irreplaceable thing you can say in 15 words about school color discipline?
