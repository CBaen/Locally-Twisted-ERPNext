# Proxy Loop 2-2 — Contestant 5 (Proof-First Buyer Suite)

## Lens: The page no one fought for

Round 2's energy went to the private page — the memorial test, the opening act
section, the KJSCOTT repositioning. All of that was right and necessary. Now look
at the civic page.

The civic page's Round 2 change was restructuring the client grid into 5 category
groupings with work-type parentheticals. That's a real proof-density upgrade. But
compared to the energy the private page received, the civic page got a structural
refinement without a voice refinement.

Here's the test: read the civic page's story block against C7's civic opening line
("When the city puts it on a banner, the balloons have to hold up"). C7's line
names the civic accountability stakes in one sentence. Your civic page's case study
intro is more editorial — "Civic events are photographed. By press. By attendees.
By city social media accounts." That's true and well-written. But it's explaining
the stakes; C7's line IS the stakes.

Your civic page has the proof density (5 grouped civic categories, parenthetical
work-type context). What it doesn't have yet is a single civic accountability
sentence that a coordinator might read once and remember two weeks later. The
category groupings tell her "you've done my kind of work." One memorable sentence
would tell her "you know what it means to do my kind of work."

This is worth a read of your civic H2. "SLC Pride, Ogden Pride, and public events
that Utah photographs" is correct and specific. Is it the sharpest possible version?
Or is there a version that names the accountability of civic decor — the way that
a failed installation isn't embarrassing for LT, it's embarrassing for the city
coordinator who hired them?

What's working: the AP/billing section isolated as its own named section on
corporate is the most structurally complete treatment of procurement friction in
the field. That move treats a real buyer hurdle as a page section, not an afterthought.
