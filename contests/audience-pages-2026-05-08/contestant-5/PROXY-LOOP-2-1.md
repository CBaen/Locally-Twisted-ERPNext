# Proxy Loop 2-1 — Contestant 5 (Proof-First Buyer Suite)

## Lens: The anti-AI-slop test

The "anti-AI-slop" test asks: where would a skeptical reader say this feels
generated? And what's the one move that proves it isn't?

For C5, the answer lives in the schools page — specifically "School colors aren't
suggestions." That H2 is the hardest line in the entire field. Not because it's
clever, but because it's specific to a specific anxiety that a specific buyer has,
expressed in the vocabulary that buyer uses in their own head. An AI optimizing for
general appeal would have written "We match your school colors exactly." Your version
has an edge. It implies the buyer has had a vendor give them "close enough" before.
It knows something about the buyer's history.

That kind of specificity — naming a lived experience rather than a feature —
is the antidote to generated-feeling copy. The question is whether the rest of
the schools page earns that H2. Does the body copy under it hold the same edge?
Or does it soften back toward feature-description after making a sharp opening?

Look at the icon bar note for "School Spirit Ready": "University red, institutional
navy — sourced to the school's official palette, not the catalog's nearest option."
That's the same voice, continued. The word "institutional" is doing real work there
— it signals that the writer knows the difference between a school's official color
system and a catalog color code.

Where the schools page might soften: the occasions grid. Six cards covering
graduation through athletic milestones. Those cards are accurate and complete, but
they read more like a capability list than like someone who knows what an activity
director's week looks like. Compare the schedule-specific copy in the story block
("7am install window before students arrive") to the occasions grid's "Setup before
guests arrive" — the story block is more specific. The occasions grid could carry
that specificity too.

What's working: KJSCOTT as the first customer voice on the private page — before
the category grid — is the most strategically placed testimonial in the field.
