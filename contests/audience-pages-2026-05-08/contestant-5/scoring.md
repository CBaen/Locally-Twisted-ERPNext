# Peer Scoring — Contestant 5

Scoring rubric:
- **Audience fit (1–10):** Does each page feel made for its specific buyer?
- **Proof density (1–10):** Does the real roster + real photo library carry believable weight?
- **Style discipline (1–10):** Does the suite obey the style guide while still bringing craft?

---

## Contestant 1 — Audience Authority

- **Audience fit: 9/10.** The operational depth is real. The corporate page's scrolling client crawl (30 names in ink) signals scale at a glance — a marketing director gets it before she reads a word. The civic vendor-documentation section (W-9, COI, PO/net-30, multi-venue) is the most operationally specific civic page in the field. Schools uses abbreviations and specific event context per named client. Private ended Round 2 with all three memorial test conditions met simultaneously — grief buyer claimed early, KJSCOTT in structural position, standalone memorial band. The only slip: the design notes acknowledge that the corporate crawl proves scale but not color precision. That gap is documented but not closed.
- **Proof density: 9/10.** 26 civic clients in a named grid, 30 corporate clients in a scrolling crawl, three named schools with specific event context, six real Google reviews on private (including the memorial review in double-deployment with purpose). Photo choices are disciplined — real installed work with civic, corporate, school, and private context. The civic Loop 2 maintainer protection block is exceptional: it explicitly guards the proof language (W-9, COI, PO) against well-intentioned softening by future build instances. That's a proof-density move that extends beyond this contest.
- **Style discipline: 9/10.** Hero contract enforced. No pastels, no off-guide fonts. Container modes declared per section. Adjacent colored band rule honored with documented verification. The scrolling crawl (CSS marquee) is the one element that tests the "no generic ecomm patterns" anti-default — the brief names carousels but not crawls, and the use here is for proof delivery, not decoration. It's defensible. Overall the suite shows more style consciousness than most.
- **Total: 27/30**

## Contestant 2 — The Right Room

- **Audience fit: 9/10.** The card-border-direction posture system is the most invisible audience-fit signal in the field — left for civic (document-like), top for corporate (header-style), bottom for schools (understated), full for private (envelope-like). Buyers won't consciously notice but the register lands. The README documents the logic well enough that a build instance can apply it correctly to new sections. The celebration-of-life standalone section ("It is not a department. It is part of the work.") is the best single line about memorial work in the field. Post-Round-2, civic gained a procurement section with screenshot-grade specificity, and schools trust pillars became concrete rather than implied.
- **Proof density: 8/10.** Named clients organized by sector for civic and corporate. Schools honestly addresses the short roster as a feature ("deep relationships, not a long shallow list") rather than padding. Private uses four real Google reviews at panel level plus a brass-ruled KJSCOTT block elevated out of the grid in Round 2. Two Odoo images confirmed-in-library with a clear plan for the implementation-phase copy. One gap: the Round 2 document says the 2×2 grid becomes three panels but the COPY.md shows only three categories listed, which is correct — but the testimonials section still has KJSCOTT even though the standalone section also has it. The double-deployment is intentional (per the notes) but it's dense, and there's a stronger argument for one structural home.
- **Style discipline: 9/10.** The border-direction system is a genuine design-system decision, not an aesthetic choice — it holds under the "why is it different" question. Container modes documented. No pastels. No off-guide fonts. Sandstone on private is palette-legal. The README explicitly documents which borders go on which audience pages — which is rare; most contestants say they did something, not how to maintain it.
- **Total: 26/30**

## Contestant 3 — Made For You (anxiety-first)

- **Audience fit: 8/10.** The anxiety-first architecture is genuinely theorized — it can be tested. The README claims a reviewer should be able to point to the anxiety-naming move on every page without instruction, and the table (civic: scale + weather + photographability; corporate: brand approximation + AP friction; schools: late vendors + schedule disruption; private: personal weight + fear of generic) holds up. The corporate H1 "On-brand. On-time. Invoice-ready." is the most compressed buyer-anxiety-as-copy in the suite. The schools page names schedule anxiety in intro copy and service cards. The weakness is the private page: Round 1 named the memorial buyer in prose but gave them card slot 5 of 6. Round 2 fixed this with a dedicated `lt-priv-memorial` section between the intro band and the grid. But the fix is architecturally lighter than C1/C2/C7/C8's Round 2 moves — slate background, two-column, KJSCOTT as callout. It's correct, but the overall private page feels slightly less emotionally inhabited than the civic and corporate pages.
- **Proof density: 8/10.** Civic has the full 26-client grid (Round 2 grouped into four categories — Cities & Counties, Pride & Equity, Chambers & Economic, Community Venues). Corporate uses 30-client chip tags for breadth. Schools has a short named roster extended by honest context items. Private relies on testimonials selected for occasion-fit with KJSCOTT in standalone structural position. The corporate chip-tag format remains flat in Round 2 — C3 held on corporate, but the field-wide note that category-grouping is superior applies here. One of the few contestants that didn't group corporate clients by sector.
- **Style discipline: 9/10.** Technically clean throughout. Hero contract verified explicitly and documented. CSS scoped to page namespace. No `!important`. No off-guide fonts. Container modes documented. The anxiety-first architecture is explicitly flagged in the README as testable — that's a style-discipline move in a different register (design reasoning made auditable). The one ambiguity: the dark authority band changes position and color depending on audience (slate for civic and corporate, navy for schools and private) — the reasoning is sound but the README doesn't explain WHY the slate/navy choice maps to those audiences, which makes it hard to maintain correctly.
- **Total: 25/30**

## Self-Reflection (Contestant 5)

My suite's strongest moves: the isolated AP/billing section on corporate is the only structural treatment in the field that addresses procurement as a standalone hurdle rather than a bullet inside a card. The civic 5-category grouping in Round 2 gave the coordinator a true peer-scan surface. "School colors aren't suggestions" is one of the two or three best H2s in the field (the other is C7's civic line). The memorial standalone section on private meets the three-condition test.

Where I fell short compared to the field: the private celebrations page spent more effort on the AP section for corporate than on the emotional architecture of the private page. In Round 1 the private page was the weakest in my suite — the Round 2 structural fix (standalone memorial opening section, KJSCOTT in pull-quote position) corrected it, but the page still feels more proof-engineered than emotionally inhabited compared to C2's celebration-of-life prose or C6's four-block architecture. C7's "When the city puts it on a banner, the balloons have to hold up." is a line I wish I had written. My civic story H2 ("When the city photographs it, the coordinator owns the result.") came close in Loop 2, but C7 found the compression first.

The scrolling client crawl (C1) and the border-direction posture system (C2) are the two structural moves I didn't have, and both are genuinely good.
